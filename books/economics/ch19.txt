@chapter 19 | Exchange Rate, FDI and External Debt

@intro
This chapter covers the **exchange rate** (flexible rates, rupee **convertibility**, **devaluation**), **remittances**, **foreign direct and portfolio investment** (sectors, sources, caps, participatory notes), **external debt** and **ECBs**, **foreign exchange reserves**, the **euro**, and the move from **FERA to FEMA**. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@table
Convertibility step | Year
Partial convertibility (LERMS) | 1992-93

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2007

(b) The FEMA

@note **FERA** (1973) treated forex offences as criminal; **FEMA** (passed **1999**, in force **1 June 2000**) made them civil and aims to **manage**, not control, foreign exchange. FEMA was "finally implemented" when FERA's sunset period ended in **2002**.
@end

@trap
**Flexible rate = market, not central bank.** **Current account convertibility 1994; Tarapore = capital account.** **Devaluation: 1949, 1966, 1991 (twice).** **India = top remittance receiver.** **Singapore = top FDI source now (Mauritius earlier).** **Services = top FDI sector.** **P-notes = FPIs.** **No FDI in lottery, gambling, chit funds.** **ECB = RBI.** **Largest debt part = commercial borrowing.** **Forex reserves: FCA > gold > SDR > IMF tranche.** **FERA → FEMA (1999/2000).** **Euro 1999; notes 2002.**
@end
