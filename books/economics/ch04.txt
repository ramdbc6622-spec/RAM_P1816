@chapter 4 | Agriculture II: Food Security, Revolutions, Prices and Credit

@intro
The second agriculture chapter covers **food security** (the NFSA, NFSM, PDS), the colour **revolutions** (Green, White, Blue, Yellow), agricultural **research and extension** (ICAR, KVKs, institutes), **crop insurance**, **marketing** (APMC, NAFED), **support prices** (MSP, CACP, FRP) and **agricultural credit** (KCC, NABARD, cooperative and land development banks). It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.

The FAO defines food security by **availability, access (physical and economic), utilisation and stability**; affordability is part of access. **Amartya Sen**'s **entitlement approach** (*Poverty and Famines*, 1981) showed famines happen when people lose the means to obtain food, even when food exists (the Bengal famine of 1943).

@table
Price term | Meaning
**MSP** | Floor price announced for **22 crops** (plus FRP for sugarcane) before sowing, on **CACP** advice, approved by the **CCEA**; since 2018 at least **1.5 times** the A2+FL cost
**CACP** | Set up in **1965** as the **Agricultural Prices Commission**; renamed 1985
**FRP** | **Fair and Remunerative Price** of **sugarcane** (since 2009), approved by the **CCEA**
**Procurement price** | Price at which the government actually **buys** grain
**Price Stabilisation Fund** | 2015; buffer stocks of **onion, potato** and pulses
@end

MSP is a safety net: if market prices are **higher**, farmers sell in the market, not to the government, and it does not guarantee "immense profit". Recent MSPs: paddy (common) **Rs. 2,369** per quintal (2025-26), wheat **Rs. 2,425** (2025-26 marketing season). **Kerala** (2020) was the first state to fix MSPs for **vegetables and fruits**.
