@chapter 8 | Taxation, GST and the Finance Commission

@intro
This chapter covers the **Goods and Services Tax** (101st Amendment, GST Council, input tax credit, GST 2.0), **direct and indirect taxes**, which government levies which tax, **VAT, MODVAT, CENVAT and service tax**, the main **sources of Central tax revenue**, **tax reform committees** (Chelliah, Kelkar), PAN and tax-saving schemes, **local body finance**, and the **Finance Commission** from K.C. Neogy to Arvind Panagariya. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

Income tax was introduced by **James Wilson** in **1860**. **Nicholas Kaldor** proposed the **expenditure tax** (1956); it was levied from 1957. The **Direct Taxes Code** (2009-10) aimed to replace the Income-tax Act 1961; that finally happened through the **Income-tax Act, 2025**, effective **1 April 2026**. Under the new regime there is no tax on income up to **Rs. 12 lakh** (Budget 2025-26).

The Finance Commission (**Article 280**) is set up by the **President** every five years. Its advice is **not binding**, and it is a **temporary** body, unlike the old Planning Commission. It decides the **vertical split** (the states' share of the divisible pool) and the **horizontal split** (among states), and recommends **grants-in-aid** (Article 275). **Agricultural income tax** belongs to the states and is never shared. UP gets the largest share of the states' pool, about **18%**.
