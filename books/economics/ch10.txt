**Demonetisation** (8 November **2016**) withdrew the **Rs. 500 and Rs. 1,000** notes. Its stated aims were to fight **black money, fake notes and terror funding**, cut the use of cash, push **digital payments** and widen the tax base; raising GDP growth was not a stated aim. About **99%** of the withdrawn notes came back. The **Rs. 2,000** note (November 2016) was withdrawn from circulation in May **2023**.

@table
Inflation type | Meaning
**Demand-pull** | Too much money chasing too few goods
**Cost-push** | Rising costs (wages, oil) push prices up
**Bottleneck** | **Supply shortages** and poor distribution; hard to tackle with demand tools
**Stagflation** | High inflation + **stagnation** (low growth, high unemployment)
**Disinflation** | Slower rise in prices
**Deflation** | Fall in the general price level
Creeping / galloping / **hyperinflation** | Below 3% / double digits / runaway
@end

**Debtors** gain from inflation (they repay in cheaper money); **creditors, savers and fixed-income earners** (pensioners) lose. To control inflation the government **raises** interest rates, curbs demand and money supply, and may ration goods; **lowering interest rates** would feed inflation.

@table
Tool | Meaning | Rate (from 5 December 2025)
**CRR** | Share of deposits kept as **cash with the RBI** | **3%**
**SLR** | Share of deposits kept by banks in **cash, gold or government securities** | **18%** (38.5% in 1991)
**Repo rate** | Rate at which **RBI lends** short-term to banks against securities | **5.25%**
**Reverse repo** | Rate at which **banks park money with the RBI** | 3.35% (fixed); the **SDF** at **5.00%** is now the floor
**Bank rate** | Rate at which RBI **rediscounts bills** / lends long-term | 5.50%
**MSF** | Emergency overnight borrowing | 5.50%
**Open market operations** | **Buying and selling government securities** | -
**Operation Twist** | Buy long-term, sell short-term securities together (from **December 2019**) to lower long-term rates | -
@end

**Quantitative tools** (bank rate, CRR, SLR, OMO) control the total amount of credit; **qualitative tools** (credit rationing, margin requirements, moral suasion) direct it. To fight inflation the RBI **raises** CRR, SLR, repo and bank rate and **sells** securities; buying securities adds money. Since **2016** a six-member **Monetary Policy Committee** sets the repo rate. **PLR** (now the MCLR/external benchmark) is set by **banks**, not the RBI. Banks, not the RBI, do **credit creation**.
