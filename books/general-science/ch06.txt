@section 6.1 | Latent Heat and Temperature Scales

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 1993
The hand burns more by steam comparatively to boiling water, because –
(a) The steam has latent heat
(b) Steam sneakes into the body
(c) The steam has more power
(d) Steam is gas
@ans (a)
@end


**Latent heat** is heat absorbed or released during a change of state without any change of temperature. Steam at 100°C carries an extra **540 cal/g** (2,260 J/g, latent heat of vaporisation) compared with water at 100°C, and gives it all up on condensing on the skin, so a steam burn is worse. Ice needs **80 cal/g** (latent heat of fusion) to melt.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2012
When ice melts, then
(a) Volume increases
(b) Volume decreases
(c) Mass increases
(d) Mass decreases
@ans (b)
@end

On heating, a body expands **in every direction** (length, breadth and volume), so its **density falls** but mass and weight stay the same. Ice is the exception at 0°C: water **contracts** when ice melts, so volume decreases. Gaps are left in railway tracks and bridges, and telephone wires sag in summer, to allow for expansion.

@section 6.3 | Humidity and Evaporation

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2024
Given below are two statements, one is labelled as
Assertion (A) and the other as Reason (R).
Assertion (A): Relative humidity decreases with increasing air temperature.
Reason (R): Absolute humidity increases with increasing evaporation.
Select the correct answer from the codes given below:
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(b) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
(c) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(d) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
@ans (a)
@end


**Relative humidity** = actual water vapour / maximum the air can hold at that temperature. Warm air can hold more vapour, so with the same moisture, RH **falls** as temperature rises. Absolute humidity rises with more evaporation. Both statements are true, but the second does not explain the first.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2008

(d) 20% RH, 60°C temperature
@ans (d)
@end

**Evaporation** is faster with **higher temperature, lower humidity, more wind and larger surface area**, and it **cools** the surface because the escaping molecules take latent heat with them.

@table
Change | Boiling point | Example
**Lower pressure** (high altitude) | Falls (about 1°C per 300 m) | Water boils near 70°C on Everest; food cooks slowly in hills
**Higher pressure** | Rises | **Pressure cooker**: about 120°C, so food cooks faster
**Dissolved impurity** (salt, sugar) | **Rises** | Salted water boils above 100°C
Dissolved impurity | Freezing point **falls** | Salt on icy roads; salt-ice mixture down to about -22°C
@end

The maximum pressure, and so the temperature, inside a cooker is set by the **weight on the vent and the area of the hole** it sits on (pressure = force/area).

@section 6.5 | Conduction, Convection and Radiation

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 2021
Which one of the following reflects back more sunlight as compared to other three?
(a) Sand Desert
(b) Paddy crop land
(c) Land covered with fresh snow
(d) Prairie land
@ans (c)
@end


Clouds act like a blanket: they absorb the heat the ground radiates at night and send part of it back, so **cloudy nights are warmer** and frost forms on clear, calm nights. **Albedo** (reflectivity) is highest for **fresh snow (80-90%)**, then desert sand (30-40%), and low for forests, crops and oceans.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2001
Assertion (A) : Woollen clothes keeps us warm.
Reason (R) : Woollen fibres are made from a special type of protein, which is a bad conductor of heat.
Select the correct answer from the codes given below:

Wool keeps us warm because its fibres trap **air**, a very poor conductor; the protein itself is not the reason, so (R) is false. A quilt warms for the same reason, and double-glazed windows and hollow bricks insulate with trapped air.

@trap
Steam burns are worse because of **latent heat (540 cal/g)**. **-40° is the same on C and F.** Ice **decreases** in volume on melting. Wet clothes dry fastest at **high temperature and low humidity**. Salt **raises** the boiling point and **lowers** the freezing point. Boiling point **falls** at altitude because **pressure falls**. Wool is warm because of **trapped air**, not protein. A cooler adds humidity; an AC removes it.
@end
