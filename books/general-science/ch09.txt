@section 9.1 | Discoveries and Basic Ideas

**Yukawa** predicted the meson (1935); **Anderson** found the positron (1932) and shared the 1936 Nobel with **Hess**, the discoverer of cosmic rays; **Bethe** explained stellar fusion (1938-39). The transuranium elements (beyond uranium) were made mainly by **Glenn Seaborg**'s team, although Fermi's 1934 neutron experiments started the search.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S.(Pre) 2009
Which one of the following is the source of renewable energy in stars?
(a) Hydrogen changes into helium
(b) Helium changes into hydrogen
(c) Decay of radioactive material
(d) Excess of oxygen which is helpful in burning and generating energy.
@ans (a)
@end

In the Sun's core (about **1.5 × 10^{7} K**), four hydrogen nuclei fuse into one **helium** nucleus, turning about 0.7% of the mass into energy (about 4 million tonnes of mass every second). **Gravitational contraction** heats a young star before fusion starts, and helps again in old age. There is no chemical burning or fission in stars.

@table
Item | Atom bomb | Hydrogen bomb | Nuclear reactor
Process | **Fission** (U-235 / Pu-239) | **Fusion** of deuterium and tritium, triggered by a fission bomb | **Controlled fission**

**Fusion reactors** (still experimental) heat hydrogen plasma to over 100 million °C inside a doughnut-shaped magnetic chamber called a **tokamak**. **ITER** in France is the international project (India is a member); India's own tokamaks are **Aditya** and **SST-1** at the Institute for Plasma Research, Gandhinagar.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2009
Enriched Uranium is
(a) Uranium sticks laid in particular shell.
(b) Natural uranium in which radioactive U235 isotope is increased artificially.
(c) mixture of natural uranium and thorium.
(d) chromium coated uranium sticks.
@ans (b)
@end

Natural uranium is 99.3% U-238 and only **0.7% U-235**, the fissile isotope. Power reactors need **3-5% U-235** (low-enriched); weapons need about 90%. India's **PHWRs** run on **natural uranium** with heavy water, which is why India could build them without enrichment. **Thorium-232** is not fissile but turns into fissile **U-233** in a reactor.

@table
Reactor part | Job | Materials
**Fuel** | Fissions to release heat | U-235 (natural or enriched), Pu-239, U-233 (from thorium)
**Moderator** | **Slows** fast neutrons so they can cause more fission | **Heavy water (D_{2}O)**, ordinary water, **graphite**
**Control rods** | **Absorb** neutrons to control the chain reaction | **Boron**, **cadmium**, hafnium
**Coolant** | Carries heat to make steam | Heavy water, ordinary water, CO_{2}, **liquid sodium** (fast reactors)
Shield | Stops radiation | Concrete, lead
@end

@table
India's three-stage programme (Homi Bhabha, 1954) | Reactor type | Fuel
Stage 1 | **Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors** (PHWR) | Natural uranium → produces plutonium
Stage 2 | **Fast Breeder Reactors** (PFBR, Kalpakkam) | Plutonium + uranium; breeds more fuel, starts thorium conversion

@end

**Research reactors** at **BARC, Trombay**: **Apsara** (1956, Asia's first), **CIRUS** (1960, closed 2010), **Dhruva** (1985), **Purnima**; and **Kamini** (Kalpakkam, uses U-233).

@trap
**Fission splits, fusion joins.** The **Sun** runs on **hydrogen → helium** fusion. **Atom bomb = fission, H-bomb = fusion**, reactor = **controlled** fission. Moderators **slow** neutrons (heavy water, graphite); control rods **absorb** them (boron, cadmium). Fast breeders use **liquid sodium**. **Harish-Chandra Research Institute is in Prayagraj**, not Chennai. **Kudankulam** has the highest capacity; **Narora** is in zone IV.
@end
