@chapter 23 | Vitamins and Nutrition

@intro
This is the single largest biology topic. It covers **vitamins**: their discovery, chemical names, sources, storage, solubility and **deficiency diseases** (beriberi, scurvy, rickets, night blindness, pellagra, anaemia). It also covers the **energy and nutrients** in food (fats, carbohydrates, proteins and amino acids, minerals such as iron and phosphorus) and **milk** (lactose, casein, carotene). It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2002

**Casimir Funk** (1912) coined the word *vitamine* ("vital amine") for the **accessory food factors** whose lack causes diseases. Vitamins are needed in tiny amounts to **regulate** metabolism; they are not killers of bacteria. **Nutraceuticals** are foods or supplements with both **nutritional and medicinal** value.

@table
Fat-soluble (stored in liver and fat) | Water-soluble (mostly not stored; extra lost in urine)
**A, D, E, K** | **B group and C**
Excess can be toxic (hypervitaminosis A and D) | Needed regularly; **vitamin C is not stored**
@end

**Vitamin B_{12}** is the exception among water-soluble vitamins: it is **stored in the liver for 3-5 years**. Being water-soluble does not explain that storage. **Vitamin D** works like a **hormone** (made in skin, activated in liver and kidney). Most vitamins (all except D, K and some B) cannot be made by the body, which is why they must be eaten.

(a) Vitamin A

**Vitamin C (ascorbic acid)** is the **antiscorbutic** vitamin: its lack causes **scurvy** (bleeding gums, slow wound healing). It helps make **collagen**, heals wounds and boosts **iron absorption**. **Amla** (Indian gooseberry) is one of the richest sources, followed by guava, chilli and citrus fruits. It is destroyed by heat and is not stored.
