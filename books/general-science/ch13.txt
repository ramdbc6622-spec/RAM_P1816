@chapter 13 | Scientists, Inventions and Physics Miscellany

@intro
This chapter closes the physics and technology unit. The first part covers **inventors and discoverers** (telescope, X-rays, radioactivity, dynamite, penicillin, the revolver), **laws and their authors** (Kepler, Newton, Pascal, Hertz), and **Indian scientists** (C.V. Raman, J.C. Bose, M.N. Saha, Homi Bhabha, Raja Ramanna, U.R. Rao, J.V. Narlikar). The miscellany covers the **Higgs boson**, lasers, nanotechnology, magnetism, liquid crystals, MRI, research laboratories, polar and deep-ocean missions, and odd devices. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 1990, 1993, 2003; U.P. U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2002
The National Science Day is observed on February 28 every year to commemorate :

The **photoelectric effect** was observed by **Heinrich Hertz** (1887); **Einstein** explained it with light quanta (photons) in 1905, which won him the **1921 Nobel Prize** (not relativity). **Kepler**'s three laws: planets move in **ellipses** with the Sun at one focus, sweep equal areas in equal times, and T^{2} ∝ r^{3}. **National Science Day (28 February)** marks C.V. Raman's announcement of the **Raman effect** in 1928.

(a) Neutrino
(b) Leptons

@ans (c)
@end


The **Higgs boson** gives other particles their mass. Peter Higgs predicted it in 1964, and CERN's **Large Hadron Collider** found it on **4 July 2012** (Nobel 2013 to Higgs and Englert). "God particle" is a media nickname. It is a **boson**, named after **S.N. Bose**.
