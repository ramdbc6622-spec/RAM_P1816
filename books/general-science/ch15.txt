@section 15.1 | Properties of Metals

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1996

(b) Iron

(d) Tungsten
@ans (d)

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2004
The heaviest natural element is ?
(a) Uranium
(b) Mercury
(c) Gold
(d) Calcium
@ans (a)
@end

Among metals, **tungsten** and **chromium** are among the hardest (diamond, a non-metal, is the hardest natural substance). **Uranium** (atomic mass 238) is the heaviest naturally abundant element; the **densest** metal is **osmium** (22.6 g/cm^{3}), followed by iridium. **Tungsten** has the highest melting point of all metals (3,422°C).

@one
Which of the following metals is in liquid state at normal temperature?
@ans Mercury
@end

**Mercury** is the only metal **liquid at room temperature** (gallium and caesium melt just above it). It is used in thermometers because it expands evenly, does not stick to glass, is opaque and shiny, and works from -39°C to 357°C. It is a good conductor of heat among liquids.

@section 15.2 | Ores and Minerals

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2016
Which one of the following metals is accessed in the native state ?
(a) Aluminium
(b) Gold
(c) Chromium
(d) Zinc
@ans (b)
@end


Metals low in the **reactivity series** (gold, platinum, sometimes silver and copper) occur **native** (free); reactive metals occur as ores. Reactivity order: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au.

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 1998
Consider these statements and choose the right answer from the given code :
Statement (A) : Normally it has been seen that iron goods covered with a brown powder called rust when they are unsheltered in the atmosphere.
Statement (R) : Rust or the brown powder is the result of deposition of tannin.
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) is correct, and (R) is right explanation of (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) is correct, but (R) is not the right explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is correct, but (R) is wrong.
(d) (A) is wrong, but (R) is correct.
@ans (c)
@end

**Rusting** needs both **oxygen and moisture** (water); rust is **hydrated iron(III) oxide** (Fe_{2}O_{3}·xH_{2}O), so the iron **gains weight**. Salt water speeds it up. **Galvanising** coats iron with **zinc**, which is more reactive and corrodes first (sacrificial protection); painting, oiling, tin-plating and alloying (stainless steel) also prevent rust.

@table
Alloy | Composition | Use
**Brass** | **Copper + zinc** | Utensils, fittings, musical instruments
**Bronze** | **Copper + tin** | Statues, medals, bells
**German silver** (nickel silver) | Copper + zinc + **nickel**; **no silver** | Cutlery, decorative items

**Solder** | **Tin + lead** (lead-free solders now use tin, silver, copper) | Joining wires
**Amalgam** | **Mercury + any metal** | Dental fillings (silver amalgam)
**Stainless steel** | Iron + **chromium** (at least 10.5%) + nickel + carbon | Utensils, surgical tools
Duralumin | Aluminium + copper + magnesium + manganese | Aircraft bodies
Magnalium | Aluminium + magnesium | Light instruments
Nichrome | Nickel + chromium | Heating elements
@end

**Magnetite** is an ore of **iron**, not aluminium, and photography uses **silver bromide**, not potassium nitrate, so only statements (i) and (iii) are true in the 2001 question.

@trap
**Tungsten** = hardest among the listed metals and highest melting point; **uranium** = heaviest natural element; **osmium** = densest. **Sodium is stored in kerosene.** **Cinnabar is mercury's ore**, not copper's. **Bauxite → aluminium.** **Gypsum** contains **calcium**. Rust **increases** iron's weight. **German silver has no silver.** **Bronze = copper + tin**, brass = copper + zinc. Stainless steel needs **chromium**, not silicon.
@end
