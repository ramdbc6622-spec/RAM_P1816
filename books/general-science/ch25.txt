@chapter 25 | Economic Importance of Plants

@intro
This chapter covers useful plants and their products: **crop varieties** (Kanchan amla, Kashi Lalima okra, Co 1148 sugarcane, Jaya rice), **petro-crops and biodiesel** (jatropha, karanja), products such as **chilgoza, caffeine, iodine from seaweed, litmus from lichen, tapa cloth**, and the **medicinal and drug plants**: cinchona and quinine, artemisinin, neem, tulsi, opium poppy, morphine, heroin and noscapine. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

**Petro-crops** are plants rich in **hydrocarbons and lipids** that can replace petroleum. **Jatropha** (ratanjot) and **karanja** (*Pongamia*, a legume) yield non-edible oil for **biodiesel**. India's first biodiesel plant came up at **Kakinada** (Andhra Pradesh). The National Policy on Biofuels (2018) focuses on non-edible oils and waste.

**Neem** (*Azadirachta indica*; "margosa") gives **azadirachtin**, a natural insect repellent, and neem-coated urea. **Tulsi**'s healing value comes from **phenols (eugenol) and flavonoids**. **Oxytocin** is a hormone, **aspartame** a synthetic sweetener and **neoprene** a synthetic rubber. @pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2016
The chief constituent of opium is–
(a) morphine
(b) heroin
(c) atropine
(d) quinine
@ans (a)
@end


**Opium** is the dried **latex of the unripe capsule** of *Papaver somniferum*, obtained by cutting the green pods. **Morphine** (about 10%) is its chief alkaloid. India is one of the few licensed opium growers (Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and **UP**, under the Narcotics Commissioner, Gwalior); the Government Opium and Alkaloid Works is at **Ghazipur** (UP).

@trap
**Kanchan = amla**, **Kashi Lalima = okra**, **Co 1148 = sugarcane**, **Jaya** = first dwarf rice of India. **Jatropha and karanja** are biodiesel plants. **Litmus from lichen**, **iodine from seaweed**, **chilgoza from pine**. **Opium = fruit latex**, **hing = stem and root resin**, **quinine = bark**, **rubber = stem latex**. **Heroin** comes from opium. **Hydroxychloroquine is synthetic.** Rayon is **cellulose**, not starch.
