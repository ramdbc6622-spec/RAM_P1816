@intro

@table
Radiation (rising frequency, falling wavelength) | Typical wavelength | Uses
**Radio waves** | Metres to km | Radio, TV broadcast, mobile phones (UHF)
**Microwaves** | mm to 30 cm | **Satellite and mobile communication**, radar, microwave ovens, Wi-Fi
**Infrared** | 780 nm - 1 mm | **TV remote**, night vision, heat lamps, thermal imaging
**Visible light** | 390-780 nm | Sight, optical fibre
**Ultraviolet** | 10-390 nm | Sterilising water, forming vitamin D, detecting forged notes
**X-rays** | 0.01-10 nm | Radiography, **CT scan**, airport baggage scanners
**Gamma rays** | Below 0.01 nm | Cancer therapy, sterilising medical equipment, food irradiation
@end

All electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light in vacuum; they differ only in frequency and wavelength. Energy rises with frequency, so **gamma rays** are the most energetic and penetrating.

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 1991; U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 1998
Long radio waves are reflected by which of the following layer of Earth's surface –
(a) Troposphere
(b) Ionosphere
(c) Tropopause
(d) Stratosphere
@ans (b)
@end


@one
Wireless signals are reflected back to the Earth's surface by the
@ans ionosphere
@end

The **ionosphere** (about 80-600 km) holds charged layers that reflect **medium and short radio waves**, which lets short-wave radio reach other continents. TV and FM signals (VHF/UHF) pass through it, so they work only up to the **horizon**: the Earth's curvature limits range, which is why TV towers are tall and satellites are used for long distances.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2005
Which one of the following statements is not true about cosmic rays?
(a) They are electromagnetic waves.
(b) They have very short wavelength.
(c) They are made of highly energetic charged particles.
(d) They originate from the Sun
@ans (a)
@end


The Sun's **short-wave** radiation passes through the air and heats the ground; the ground then radiates **long-wave (infrared) terrestrial radiation**, which the air, water vapour and CO_{2} absorb. So the atmosphere is heated mainly **from below**. **Cosmic rays** are not electromagnetic waves but high-energy **charged particles** (mostly protons) from the Sun and distant supernovae.

(a) Infrared Rays
(b) Ultrasonic Waves
(c) Visible Light
(d) X-Rays
@ans (d)

Satellite TV uses microwaves of a few centimetres. **Heavy rain** both **absorbs** and **scatters** them ("rain fade"), and a small dish has little margin to compensate, so all three statements are accepted. @pyq U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2002
Two astronauts cannot hear each other on the Moon's surface, because :
(a) Their ears have stopped working on the Moon.
(b) No atmosphere on the Moon.
(c) They wear special space suits on the Moon.
(d) The sound travels much more slowly on Moon.
@ans (b)
@end


@one
An astronaut cannot hear his companion on the Moon's surface because
@ans there is no medium for the transmission of sound.
@end

@table
Medium | Speed of sound (approx.)
Vacuum | Zero: sound cannot travel
Air (0°C) | 332 m/s (about 343 m/s at 20°C)
Water | About 1,480 m/s
**Steel** | About 5,000-6,000 m/s
@end

Sound is a **mechanical, longitudinal** wave that needs a medium, so it travels **fastest in solids**, slower in liquids and slowest in gases (**V_{air} < V_{water} < V_{steel}**). Its speed in air **rises with temperature** and humidity but does not depend on pressure. Astronauts on the airless Moon talk by radio.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2025
Given below are two statements, one is labelled as
Assertion (A) and the other as Reason (R).

Reason (R) : Length and thickness of vocal cord is different in men and women.
Select the correct answer from the code given below :
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(b) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.

**Pitch** depends on **frequency**. Men's vocal cords are usually **longer and thicker** and vibrate more slowly, so their voices are deeper; women's and children's voices have higher pitch. **Loudness** depends on amplitude and **quality (timbre)** on the overtones present.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2007
To hear a clear echo, the minimum distance between the reflecting surface and the observer should be :
(a) 165 feet
(b) 165 metre
(c) 16.5 feet
(d) 16.5 metre
@ans (d)
@end

The ear keeps a sound for about **1/10 second**, so an echo is heard separately only if it returns at least 0.1 s later. In 0.1 s sound travels about 33-34 m, there and back, so the wall must be at least **about 17 m** away (16.5 m at 0°C). **Reverberation** is the persistence of sound in a hall from repeated reflections; concert halls use **sound-absorbing** curtains, carpets, panels and false ceilings to control it.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S.(Pre) 2013; U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2008
Sound above what level (in decibels) is considered hazardous noise pollution?
(a) 30 dB
(b) 100 dB
(c) 80 dB
(d) 120 dB
@ans (c)
@end


The **decibel** scale is logarithmic: every **10 dB** means ten times the intensity. A whisper is about 30 dB, conversation 60 dB, heavy traffic 80-90 dB and a jet take-off 120-140 dB (pain threshold about 120 dB). Long exposure above **85 dB** can cause permanent hearing loss.

@section 7.5 | Musical Instruments, Remote Controls and Television

@pyq U.P. R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) 2021
In a Sitar, which type of sound vibrations are produced?
(a) Progressive and Longitudinal
(b) Progressive and Transverse
(c) Stationary and Longitudinal
(d) Stationary and Transverse
@ans (d)
@end


A plucked sitar or guitar string vibrates in **stationary (standing), transverse** waves; the air column of a flute vibrates in stationary **longitudinal** waves.

@trap
**Radio < microwave < infrared < visible < UV < X-ray < gamma** in frequency. **Cosmic rays are particles**, not EM waves. The atmosphere is heated by **long-wave terrestrial** radiation. Sound is fastest in **steel**, and cannot travel in vacuum. Echo needs **about 17 m**. Ultrasound is **above 20 kHz**. TV remotes use **infrared**. Silence zone limit = **50 dB** by day.
@end
