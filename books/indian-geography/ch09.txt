@chapter 9 | Climate and the Monsoon

@intro
India has a **tropical monsoon climate**: winds reverse with the seasons, and most rain falls in four months. The **south-west monsoon** (June to September) gives about **75%** of the annual rain; the long-period average for the country is about **87 cm** for the season and about 116 cm for the year. In winter the winds blow off the land from the north-east and are mostly dry, except on the **Coromandel coast** (north-east monsoon) and in the north-west (**western disturbances**). The **Himalaya** shapes all of this: it shuts out cold Central Asian air and forces the monsoon to drop its rain over the subcontinent. This chapter covers the monsoon mechanism, its arrival and branches, regional rainfall, winter rain, local storms and seasons. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 9.1 | India as a Monsoon Land and the Role of the Himalaya

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1996, 1998; U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 1998
Assertion(A) : India is a Monsoonal Country.
Reason (R) : The high Himalayas impart it climatic distinctiveness.
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
@ans (a)
@end

The Himalaya acts as a **climatic divide**. It blocks the cold, dry winds of Central Asia, so north India is warmer in winter than other places at the same latitude. It also traps the monsoon winds, which then rise and rain over the plains and the hills instead of crossing into Tibet. Without the range, north India would be a cold desert.

The **southern slopes** face the monsoon and the sun, so they are wetter, warmer and more densely forested. The northern slopes lie in the rain shadow and are colder and drier. @pyq U.P. R.O./A.R.O. (Mains) 2016
Mansoon originates from which of the following winds in India?
(a) South-West winds
(b) South-East winds
(c) North-East winds
(d) North-West winds
@ans (a)
@end

The south-east **trade winds** of the southern hemisphere cross the Equator, are turned to the right by the Coriolis force and reach India as **south-west winds**. They are drawn by the low pressure over north-west India and the northward shift of the **ITCZ**, helped by the **tropical easterly jet** and the shift of the subtropical westerly jet. The monsoon normally sets in over **Kerala on 1 June** (2025: 24 May, the earliest since 2009) and covers the whole country by **8 July**. It withdraws from the north-west from about 17 September.

@table
Branch | Path | Effect
Arabian Sea branch | Strikes the Western Ghats | Heavy rain on the windward side (Konkan, Malabar); rain shadow on the Deccan
Arabian Sea branch (north) | Gujarat, Rajasthan along the Aravalli | Little rain: the Aravalli lies parallel to the wind
Bay of Bengal branch | North-east hills (Garo, Khasi) | World's heaviest rain at Mawsynram and Cherrapunji
Bay of Bengal branch (west) | Deflected west by the Himalaya along the Ganga plain | Rain falls from east (heavy) to west (lighter)
@end

@section 9.3 | Rainfall: Highest, Lowest and Regional Patterns

(a) Arunachal Pradesh
(b) Sikkim
(c) Kerala
(d) Jammu and Kashmir
@ans (b)
@note in the IMD figures for 2024 Sikkim (about 2,799 mm) is just ahead of Kerala (about 2,795 mm), with Arunachal Pradesh at about 2,193 mm. The margin is small and changes from year to year; **Meghalaya** and **Goa** usually have the highest state averages.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2010; U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2008, 2009
Among the following places which one gets the least rainfall?
(a) Leh
(b) Bikaner
(c) Jaisalmer
(d) Cherrapunji
@ans (a)
@end

**Leh** lies in the rain shadow of the Himalaya and the Zaskar range and gets less than 10 cm a year, mostly as snow. **Jaisalmer** (about 20 cm) is the driest place in the Thar, and **Bikaner** gets about 30 cm. **Mawsynram** (over 11,000 mm) and **Cherrapunji (Sohra)** in Meghalaya are the wettest places in the world.

@table
Annual rainfall | Regions
Over 200 cm | West coast and Western Ghats, Meghalaya and the north-east, Andaman and Nicobar, sub-Himalayan West Bengal
100 to 200 cm | Eastern India, east of the Peninsula, the middle Ganga plain
50 to 100 cm | Western UP, Punjab, Haryana, interior Deccan, Gujarat, east Rajasthan
Below 50 cm | West Rajasthan, Kutch, Ladakh, rain-shadow Deccan pockets
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2019
The winter rains caused by Western disturbances in North Western Plain of India gradually decrease from:
(a) East to West
(b) West to East
(c) North to South
(d) South to North
@ans (b)
@end

**Western disturbances** are low-pressure systems that start over the **Mediterranean** and move east with the **subtropical westerly jet**. They bring winter rain to Punjab, Haryana and western UP and snow to the western Himalaya. Their effect fades **from west to east**. This light winter rain (**mahawat**) is very useful for the **rabi** crops, especially wheat.

Winter rain in the **north-west** comes from **western disturbances**; in the **south-east** (Tamil Nadu) from the **north-east monsoon**. Never cross the two. The "retreating monsoon" is the same October-December season seen from the south.
