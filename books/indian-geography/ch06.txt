@chapter 6 | Drainage I: The Himalayan Rivers
@unit UNIT II · DRAINAGE, CLIMATE AND NATURAL ENVIRONMENT

@intro
India's rivers fall into two groups. The **Himalayan rivers** (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra and their tributaries) are fed by snow and glaciers as well as rain, so they are **perennial**. They are young, cut deep gorges, carry huge loads of silt and build wide plains and deltas. The **Peninsular rivers** (Chapter 7) are rain-fed, older and follow graded valleys. This chapter covers the nature of Himalayan rivers, the Ganga and its Himalayan headwaters, the Yamuna and the other tributaries, the Brahmaputra and the Indus system. UPPSC asks the Ganga system most often, since nearly all of Uttar Pradesh lies in its basin. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@table
Type | Meaning | Indian examples
Antecedent | River older than the mountain; it kept cutting down as the land rose | Indus, Satluj, Brahmaputra (Dihang), Ganga headwaters, Kosi
Consequent | Follows the original slope of the land | Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri (east-flowing Peninsular rivers)

@one U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 2020
Which one of the Rivers is a Trans-Himalayan river?
@ans Sutlej
@end

The **Satluj** rises at **Rakas Tal**, beside Mansarovar in Tibet, north of the Himalaya. It enters India at **Shipki La** and cuts through the Zaskar, Greater, Lesser and Shiwalik ranges in deep gorges. It is therefore both **trans-Himalayan** and **antecedent**. The Indus and Brahmaputra are the other two trans-Himalayan rivers.

@trap
In Assertion-Reason items, match the Reason to the **dry-season** flow. Snow-melt explains perenniality; monsoon rain does not. Of the Indus tributaries in India, only the **Satluj** rises beyond the Himalaya.
@end

@section 6.2 | The Ganga and its Himalayan Headwaters

The **Ganga** (2,525 km) rises as the **Bhagirathi** at **Gaumukh**, the snout of the Gangotri glacier, in Uttarkashi. It takes the name Ganga at **Devprayag** and enters the plains at **Haridwar**. **Uttar Pradesh** has the longest stretch of the river (about 1,450 km). It was declared India's **National River** in 2008.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2016

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2019
'Moribund Delta' is a sub-division of which of the following Deltas?
(a) Krishna-Godawari Delta
(b) Mahanadi Delta
(c) Bengal Delta
(d) Cauvery Delta
@ans (c)
@end

The **Ganga-Brahmaputra (Bengal) delta** is the largest delta in the world. Its western part, served by the Bhagirathi-Hooghly, gets little fresh water now that the main flow has moved east. Rivers there are silting up and dying, so it is called the **moribund delta**. The eastern part, where the Padma and Meghna still build land, is the **active delta**. The **Sundarbans** occupy its seaward edge.

@section 6.4 | Ken-Betwa Link and Rivers of Bundelkhand

(a) Brahmaputra and Ganga
(b) Tapi and Beas

The **Indus** (from Bokhar Chu near Mansarovar), the **Satluj** (Rakas Tal) and the **Brahmaputra** (Chemayungdung glacier) all rise near **Mount Kailash and Lake Mansarovar**. The Indus flows west and the Brahmaputra east, so the two enclose the whole Himalaya.

The Brahmaputra (about 2,900 km) carries one of the largest silt loads in the world. In Assam it forms **Majuli**, the largest river island. China's large dam project on the Yarlung Tsangpo at Medog (started 2025) is a current concern for India.
