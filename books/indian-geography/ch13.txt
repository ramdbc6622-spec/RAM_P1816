@chapter 13 | Irrigation and Canals
@unit UNIT III · WATER, AGRICULTURE AND LIVESTOCK

@intro
Only about half of India's cropped area is irrigated, and **groundwater** (tube wells and wells) now supplies more than 60% of it. Canals come second, and **tanks** still matter in the hard-rock south. Irrigation is highest in **Punjab and Haryana** (over 90% of cropped area) and lowest in the hill states and rain-fed plateau. **Uttar Pradesh** has the largest irrigated area of any state, mostly by tube wells. This chapter covers sources of irrigation, regional patterns, the great canals and irrigation concepts. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 13.1 | Sources of Irrigation and Regional Patterns

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2015
Given below are two statements. One is Assertion (A) and the other is Reason (R) :
Assertion (A) : One of the major sources of irrigation in Peninsular India is a pond.
Reason (R) : Most of the rivers in the Peninsular region are seasonal.
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are correct and (R) explains (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) are correct but (R) does not explain (A).
(c) (A) is correct, but (R) is false.
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is correct.
@ans (a)
@end

In the Peninsula, hard crystalline rock holds little groundwater and makes wells and canals costly, and rivers dry up after the monsoon. Rain is therefore stored in **tanks** (ponds) behind small earthen bunds. Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Karnataka have thousands of them; Telangana's **Mission Kakatiya** (2015) revived old tanks.

@table
Source | Share of net irrigated area (approx.) | Leading regions
Tube wells and other wells | About 64% | UP, Punjab, Haryana, Bihar, Gujarat, Rajasthan
Canals | About 24% | Punjab, Haryana, UP, Rajasthan (IGNP), Andhra Pradesh
Tanks | About 3% | Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka
Other sources | About 9% | Lift, springs, small storages
@end

(a) Uttar Pradesh

UP link: about **three-quarters** of UP's net irrigated area is watered by **tube wells**, under a fifth by canals and a little by ponds and other sources. The state's flat alluvium, shallow water table and rural electrification (and now solar pumps under **PM-KUSUM**) explain the spread.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1995
During last 25 years, the development of tubewell irrigation has been most spectacular in :
(a) Ganga-Ghaghra Doab
(b) Ganga-Yamuna Doab
(c) Saryupar Plain
(d) Bundelkhand
@ans (c)
@end

UP link: the **Saryupar plain** (north of the Ghaghara-Saryu: Gorakhpur, Basti, Deoria, Maharajganj) had almost no canals, so the growth of tube wells there from the 1970s was the most striking in the state. The Ganga-Yamuna doab already had the Upper and Lower Ganga canals, and Bundelkhand's hard rock limits wells.

@pyq U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Mains) 2010
Life saving or Protective irrigation refers to:
(a) Constant flow of water
(b) Irrigation at critical growth stage
(c) P.W.P. Irrigation
(d) Alternate wetting and drying
@ans (c)
@end

**Protective (life-saving) irrigation** is a single watering given when soil moisture falls close to the **permanent wilting point (PWP)**, the level below which plants wilt beyond recovery. It saves a rain-fed crop in a dry spell rather than raising yields. **Alternate wetting and drying** is a water-saving method for rice.

@end

@one U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1999
Indira Gandhi Canal has its source at :
@ans Harike Barrage
@end

@one U.P.P.S.C. (GIC) 2010
Indira Gandhi Canal receives its water supply from
@ans Beas, Ravi and Satluj
@end

The **Indira Gandhi Canal** (Rajasthan Canal) starts at the **Harike barrage**, just below the meeting of the **Satluj and Beas** in Punjab. The Ravi's water reaches it through the Ravi-Beas link. At about 650 km including its feeder, it is India's longest canal and irrigates the north-western Thar (Sri Ganganagar, Hanumangarh, Bikaner, Jaisalmer).

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1999
The Garland Canal System in India was proposed by :
(a) K.L. Rao
(b) Dineshaw J. Dastur
(c) Visvesvaraya
(d) Y.K. Alagh
@ans (b)
@end

Captain **Dinshaw J. Dastur** proposed the **Garland Canal** (1977): two linked canals, one along the Himalaya and one around the Peninsula. Dr **K.L. Rao**, then Irrigation Minister, had earlier (1972) proposed a **National Water Grid** linking the Ganga and the Kaveri. Neither was built, but the idea led to the **National Perspective Plan (1980)** and the present river-linking programme (Ken-Betwa, Chapter 6).
