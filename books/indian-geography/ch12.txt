@chapter 12 | Natural Vegetation and Forests

@intro
India's natural vegetation follows rainfall and altitude. Wet regions carry **tropical evergreen** forest, the monsoon belt carries **deciduous** forest (the largest type, with teak and sal), dry regions carry **thorn scrub**, and the Himalaya goes from tropical to temperate, coniferous and **alpine** belts as height rises. The **India State of Forest Report 2023** puts forest cover at about **21.8%** and forest plus tree cover at about **25.2%** of the country, against the national goal of 33%. This chapter covers forest types and their trees, the Himalayan tree line, mangroves and social forestry. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 12.1 | Forest Types and their Trees

@table
Forest type (after Champion and Seth) | Rainfall | Areas | Typical trees
Tropical evergreen | Over 200 cm | Western Ghats, north-east, Andaman and Nicobar | Rosewood, mahogany, ebony, cinchona
Tropical moist deciduous | 100 to 200 cm | Tarai, north-east Peninsula, eastern slopes of the Western Ghats | Sal, teak, shisham, sandalwood
Tropical dry deciduous | 70 to 100 cm | Middle Ganga plain, central Deccan | Teak, sal, peepal, neem
Tropical thorn | Below 70 cm | Rajasthan, Gujarat, rain-shadow Deccan | Khejri, babool, kikar, cactus
Montane (temperate and coniferous) | Varies with height | Himalaya above 1,500 m, Nilgiri sholas | Deodar, chir pine, oak, fir, spruce
Alpine | Above about 3,600 m | Higher Himalaya | Juniper, birch, rhododendron; alpine meadows
Littoral and swamp (mangrove) | Tidal | Deltas and coasts | Sundari, rhizophora, avicennia
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2007
Teak and Sal are the products of –
(a) Tropical Dry Deciduous Forests
(b) Tropical Evergreen Forests
(c) Tropical Thorn Forests
(d) Alpine Forests
@ans (a)
@end

**Deciduous (monsoon) forests** cover about two-thirds of India's forest area, the dry type being the most widespread. **Teak** and **sal** grow in both the moist and the dry deciduous types, so (a) is the only option that fits.

**Deodar** (*Cedrus deodara*) grows in the **western Himalaya** between about 1,500 and 3,000 m; it is the state tree of Himachal Pradesh. The **Nilgiris** carry **shola** forests and grasslands, with planted eucalyptus and wattle. **Sal** (*Shorea robusta*) dominates the north-eastern Peninsula (Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh) and the Tarai; **teak** the western and central Peninsula.

UP link: UP's natural forests are **sal** forests of the Tarai (Dudhwa, Katarniaghat, Suhelwa, Pilibhit) and dry deciduous and thorn forests of **Bundelkhand** and the **Vindhyan** belt. UP's forest cover is only about 6% of its area, one of the lowest among large states.

@pyq U.P.P.S.C. (GIC) 2010
In which one of the following states Cinchona trees does not grow?
(a) Assam
(b) Kerala
(c) Chhattisgarh
(d) West Bengal
@ans (c)
@end

**Cinchona**, the source of **quinine**, needs a warm, very wet climate and acidic soil. Government plantations were set up at **Mungpoo** (Darjeeling, West Bengal) and in the **Nilgiris**. Kerala and the north-east are also suitable. Dry Chhattisgarh is not.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1991
Which of the following plants have no flowers?
(a) Jackfruit
(b) Fig
(c) Orchid

**Ferns** are seedless vascular plants that reproduce by **spores**. The **fig** does flower, but its tiny flowers are hidden inside the fruit-like **syconium**, which is why it is a favourite trap option.

@section 12.2 | The Himalayan Tree Line

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Mains) 2005
Compared to the Eastern Himalayas, the height of the tree-line in the Western Himalayas is:
(a) More
(b) Less
(c) Same
(d) Unrelated variable
@ans (b)
@end

The **tree line** is the height above which trees cannot grow. In the **eastern Himalaya** it lies at about 3,600 to 4,000 m because the region is closer to the Tropic, gets more rain and is warmer at each height. In the **western Himalaya**, further north and drier, it falls to about 3,000 to 3,300 m. The **snow line** shows the same east-high, west-low pattern.

@section 12.3 | Social Forestry and Mangroves

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2002
An example of a multipurpose tree used in social forestry is –
(a) Sheesham
(b) Dhak
(c) Eucalyptus
(d) Khejri
@ans (d)
@end

**Khejri** (*Prosopis cineraria*), the "king of the desert" and state tree of Rajasthan, gives fodder, fuel, food (sangri pods) and shade, and fixes nitrogen. In **1730** the **Bishnoi** community of **Khejarli** (Jodhpur), led by **Amrita Devi**, gave their lives to save khejri trees. **Social forestry** (from the 1976 National Commission on Agriculture) means planting on community and waste land for local needs.

Which one of the following mangrove areas is situated in the delta regions of Godavari River?

(c) Malpe

@table

Sundarbans | West Bengal | Ganga-Brahmaputra delta; largest; Sundari tree; tigers
Bhitarkanika | Odisha | Brahmani-Baitarani delta; saltwater crocodiles
Coringa | Andhra Pradesh | Godavari delta near Kakinada
Krishna mangroves | Andhra Pradesh | Krishna delta
Pichavaram | Tamil Nadu | Between the Vellar and Coleroon (Kaveri) estuaries

Vembanad-Kochi | Kerala | Backwater mangroves

India has about **4,992 sq km** of mangroves (ISFR 2023), about 0.15% of its area; **West Bengal** has the most, followed by **Gujarat** and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. **Coondapoor (Kundapura), Honnavar** and **Malpe** are estuary towns on the Karnataka coast.

@trap
**Vembanad** is a Kerala lake with backwater mangroves; it is never in Karnataka. The Godavari delta mangrove is **Coringa**; the Kaveri delta mangrove is **Pichavaram**.
@end
