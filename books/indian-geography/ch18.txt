@chapter 18 | Silk, Plantation Crops and Spices

@intro
India is the world's **second largest producer of silk** (after China) and the only country that produces all four commercial silks: **mulberry, tasar, eri and muga**. Its plantation crops (**tea, coffee, rubber**, coconut, cashew and spices) grow on hill slopes and coasts with heavy rain, and each has its own **commodity board**. **Kerala** is the "garden of spices", **Assam** grows half of India's tea, and **Karnataka** leads in coffee and silk. Several leading states have changed in recent years, which affects the keys. This chapter covers silk, tea, coffee, rubber, tobacco, coconut, cashew and spices. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 18.1 | Silk

@table
Type of silk | Silkworm feeds on | Leading state
Mulberry (about 70% of output) | Mulberry leaves | Karnataka, then Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu
Tasar (tropical) | Arjun, asan, sal | Jharkhand, then Chhattisgarh, Odisha
Eri | Castor leaves | Assam, then Meghalaya
Muga (golden) | Som and soalu trees | Assam (over 80%), Meghalaya; found only in India
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S (Pre) 2010
In an area with an annual rainfall of more than 200 cms and sloping hills which crop will be ideal?
(a) Jute
(b) Cotton
(c) Tea
(d) Maize
@ans (c)
@end

**Tea** needs warm, humid weather, **150 to 300 cm** of well-spread rain, and **sloping**, well-drained, **acidic** soil so that water does not stand at the roots. **Assam** (Brahmaputra and Barak valleys) grows about half of India's tea, followed by **West Bengal** (Darjeeling, Dooars, Terai), **Tamil Nadu** (Nilgiris) and **Kerala**.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2008

(a) First
(b) Second
(c) Third
(d) Fourth
@ans (a)
@note in 2007-08 India was first in both. **China** has since moved ahead in production and consumption; India is now **second** in both and among the top three exporters (with Kenya and China).
@end

India grows both **arabica** and **robusta**, the latter in larger quantity.

@section 18.4 | Plantation Agriculture, Tobacco and Coconut

@pyq U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2001
The main crop cultivated in India under plantation agriculture is –
(a) Tea, Rubber, coconut, coffee
(b) Tea, Rubber, Sunflower, Soyabean
(c) Tea, Banana, Grapes, Coconut
(d) Tea, Rubber, Coconut, Soyabean
@ans (a)
@end

**Plantation agriculture** grows a single perennial cash crop on large estates with much capital, hired labour and on-site processing, mostly for market or export. In India it began with British tea estates in Assam (1830s). Soybean and sunflower are seasonal field crops.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2012

(a) Gujarat
(b) Kerala
(c) Madhya Pradesh
(d) Punjab
@ans (c)
@end

**Kerala** is the "garden of spices" by variety (pepper, cardamom, ginger, nutmeg, clove) and leads in **small cardamom**. By total **quantity**, **Madhya Pradesh** is the largest spice producer (garlic, coriander, chilli), followed by Gujarat and Andhra Pradesh. **Karnataka** has recently overtaken Kerala in **black pepper**. Large cardamom comes from **Sikkim**.

@pattern
Plantation and silk items are "leading state" questions, so the data year matters more here than anywhere else in the book. Eight items have a key that is correct for the exam year but not today; each carries a note. Fixed answers worth memorising: coffee in Karnataka (about 70%), rubber in Kerala, tea in Assam, mulberry silk in Karnataka, tasar in Jharkhand, eri and muga in Assam, and the board headquarters. UP's links are Banarasi silk weaving and chewing tobacco.
