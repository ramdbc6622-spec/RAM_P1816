@chapter 20 | Mineral Resources I: Metallic and Non-metallic Minerals
@unit UNIT IV · MINERALS, ENERGY AND INDUSTRY

@intro
India's minerals are concentrated in the old rocks of the **Peninsular plateau**, above all in the **Chotanagpur-Odisha-Chhattisgarh** belt, the **Karnataka-Goa** belt and the **Aravalli** of Rajasthan. The **Dharwar** system holds most metallic ores, the **Vindhyan** system limestone and sandstone, and the **Gondwana** system nearly all the coal (Chapter 21). UPPSC asks minerals mainly through **mine-mineral-state matching** and "largest producer" questions, and since 2023 through **critical minerals** such as lithium. This chapter covers rock systems, iron ore, ferro-alloys, non-ferrous metals, mica and building stones, atomic minerals, and mixed matching. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 20.2 | Critical Minerals

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2023
With respect to mineral resources, which of the following statement/s is/are correct ?
1. Lithium's inferred resources have been found in Reasi district of Jammu & Kashmir.
2. India is import dependent for many minerals like Lithium, Nickel and Cobalt.
Code-
(a) Only 1
(b) Only 2
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2
@ans (c)
@end

In **February 2023** the Geological Survey of India reported about **5.9 million tonnes** of inferred lithium resources at **Salal-Haimana, Reasi**. India imports almost all its lithium, cobalt and nickel. The government has listed **30 critical minerals** (2023) and launched the **National Critical Mineral Mission** (2025); the public company **KABIL** acquires lithium assets abroad (for example in Argentina).

@heatmap

@pattern
This is the largest single block of questions in the book. About two-thirds are matching items: mine to mineral, mine to state, mineral to leading state. Six place names solve most codes: **Khetri** (copper), **Kodarma** (mica), **Kudremukh** and **Bailadila** (iron), **Zawar** (zinc-lead-silver), **Jaduguda** (uranium). "Largest producer" keys have shifted for mica, manganese, graphite and asbestos, and three pairs moved to Telangana in 2014 (Khammam, Lambapur and Singareni in Chapter 21). The 2023 paper added lithium and critical minerals.
