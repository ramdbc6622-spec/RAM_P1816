@chapter 10 | Natural Disasters

@intro
India is among the most disaster-prone countries in the world. About **58.6%** of its land is prone to earthquakes of moderate to very high intensity, about 12% (over 4 crore hectares) to floods, about 68% of cultivated land to drought, and about three-quarters of its 7,500 km coastline to cyclones and tsunamis. The **Disaster Management Act, 2005** set up the **National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)**, chaired by the Prime Minister, and the **National Disaster Response Force (NDRF)**. This chapter covers tsunamis and cyclones, earthquakes and seismic zones, landslides, floods and the institutions for warning and response. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 10.1 | Tsunamis and Cyclones

@pyq U.P.R.O./A.R.O (Mains) 2014
Which of the following coasts of India was worst affected by the 2004 Tsunami?
(a) Malabar Coast
(b) Konkan Coast
(c) Coromandel Coast
(d) Northern Circars Coast
@ans (c)
@end

The **26 December 2004** tsunami was set off by a magnitude 9.1 earthquake off northern Sumatra. In India it struck the **Coromandel coast** of Tamil Nadu (Nagapattinam worst of all) and the **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**. Mangrove-lined coasts such as Pichavaram suffered less.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2012
Tsunami Warning Centre in India is located in
(a) Chennai
(b) Vishakhapatnam
(c) Hyderabad
(d) Port Blair
@ans (c)
@end

The **Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC)** was set up in **2007** at the **Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS), Hyderabad**, under the Ministry of Earth Sciences. It can issue a warning within about 10 minutes of a large undersea earthquake and also serves as a regional centre for the Indian Ocean.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2012
Indian Meteorological Department is established at:
(a) New Delhi
(b) Nagpur
(c) Jodhpur
(d) Pune
@ans (a)
@end

The **India Meteorological Department** was founded in **1875** at Calcutta. Its headquarters moved to Shimla (1905) and Pune (1928), and to **New Delhi** in 1944. It celebrated its 150th year in 2025. It names and tracks cyclones in the north Indian Ocean as a WMO Regional Specialised Meteorological Centre.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2003
Assertion (A) : East coast is most prone to cyclones than western coast.
Reason (R) : Eastern coast of India lies in the zone of north-east trade winds.
In the context of above statements, which of the following is correct?
Code :
(a) (A) and (R) both are true, and (R) is correct explanation of (A).
(b) (A) and (R) both are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
(d) (R) is true, but (A) is false.
@ans (b)
@end

The **Bay of Bengal** is warmer and shallower than the Arabian Sea, and the remnants of Pacific typhoons often enter it from the east. It forms about four times as many cyclones, and the low, flat deltaic east coast lets storm surges travel far inland. The trade winds are not the cause.

@pyq U.P.R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) 2021
Cyclone "Tauktae" was formed in which ocean?
(a) Bay of Bengal
(b) Indian Ocean
(c) Caspian Sea
(d) Arabian Sea
@ans (d)
@end

**Tauktae** (name given by Myanmar, meaning "gecko") formed in the **Arabian Sea** in May 2021 and hit the **Saurashtra coast of Gujarat** as an extremely severe cyclone. The Arabian Sea is warming fast, so severe cyclones there (Tauktae 2021, Biparjoy 2023) are becoming more frequent.

@section 10.2 | Earthquakes and Seismic Zones

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2006
Which one of the following areas of India does not come under the zone of high seismic intensity ?
(a) Uttarakhand
(b) Karnataka Plateau
(c) Kutch
(d) Himachal Pradesh
@ans (b)
@end

@one U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2013
India has been divided into how many Seismic Risk Zones?
@ans 4
@end

@table
Zone (IS 1893: 2002) | Intensity | Zone factor | Main areas
V (very high) | IX and above | 0.36 | North-east, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal, Uttarakhand, Kutch, north Bihar, Andaman and Nicobar (about 11% of land)
IV (high) | VIII | 0.24 | Delhi, rest of the Himalayan foothills, Indo-Gangetic plain including most of western and northern UP (about 18%)
III (moderate) | VII | 0.16 | Kerala, Goa, Lakshadweep, much of central India, Chennai, Mumbai (about 30%)
II (low) | VI or less | 0.10 | Most of the Peninsular plateau, Karnataka, Hyderabad (about 41%)
@end

The 2002 map has **four zones (II to V)**; the old zone I was merged into zone II. The revised code adopted by the **Bureau of Indian Standards in 2025** adds a new highest zone, **VI**, for the whole Himalayan arc.

@pyq U.P.P.S.C. (R.I.) 2014

@pyq U.P.P.C.S.(Pre) 2001
Assertion (A) : The Koyna region of Maharashtra is likely to become more earthquake prone in near future.
Reason (R) : The Koyna dam is located on an old fault-plane which may get activated more frequently with changes in water-level in Koyna reservoir.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not a correct explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
@ans (a)
@end

**Koyna** is the classic Indian case of **reservoir-induced seismicity**. After the dam was filled, a magnitude 6.3 earthquake struck in **December 1967**, the largest known quake of this kind. The weight of water and the rise in pore pressure along old faults trigger tremors.

Major earthquake | Year | Magnitude (approx.)

Bihar-Nepal | 1934 | 8.0
Assam-Tibet (Medog) | 1950 | 8.6
Koyna (Maharashtra) | 1967 | 6.3
Uttarkashi | 1991 | 6.8
Latur (Killari, Maharashtra) | 1993 | 6.2
Bhuj (Gujarat) | 2001 | 7.7
Kashmir (Muzaffarabad) | 2005 | 7.6
Sikkim | 2011 | 6.9
@end

@trap
Seismic **zone II** is the **lowest** risk, zone V the highest; "zone I" no longer exists in the 2002 map. A city in the Peninsula (Hyderabad, Bengaluru) can never be zone IV or V. Remember that even "stable" Latur and Koyna on the plateau have had damaging earthquakes.
@end

@section 10.3 | Landslides, Floods and Disaster-prone States

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2003
Given below are two statements. One is labelled as Assertion and other as Reason :
Assertion (A) : The frequency of landslides has increased in the Himalayas.
Reason (R) : There has been large scale mining in the Himalayas in recent years.
In the context of the above which one of the following is correct :
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
(c) (A) is true but (R) is false
(d) (A) is false but (R) is true
@ans (a)
@end

Landslides in the young, steep and fractured Himalaya are rising with road cutting, tunnelling, mining, deforestation and heavier rain bursts. **Joshimath** (Uttarakhand) began sinking visibly in **January 2023**, and the **Wayanad** (Kerala) landslides of **July 2024** killed over 200 people. ISRO's **Landslide Atlas of India (2023)** ranks Rudraprayag and Tehri among the most exposed districts.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2000
The most flood-prone state of India is :
(a) Assam
(b) Andhra Pradesh
(c) Bihar
(d) Uttar Pradesh
@ans (c)
@end

**Bihar** has about **73%** of its area flood-prone. Its northern rivers (Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati, Burhi Gandak) bring floods from Nepal almost every year. By total area, **Uttar Pradesh** has the largest flood-prone area of any state (about 73 lakh hectares), and Assam suffers the longest and most frequent floods.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2012
Among the following area of Uttar Pradesh which is maximum flood affected ?
(a) Western area
(b) Eastern area
(c) Middle area
(d) Northern area
@ans (b)
@end

UP link: **eastern UP** (Gorakhpur, Maharajganj, Kushinagar, Basti, Bahraich, Gonda, Ballia) suffers most. The Rapti, Ghaghara, Gandak and Sharda bring Himalayan floods from Nepal, the land is flat, and the rivers often break their embankments. Western UP floods mainly along the Yamuna and Hindon.

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2002
Which among the following states faces a maximum natural disaster?
(a) Andhra Pradesh
(b) Odisha
(c) Bihar
(d) Gujarat
@ans (b)
@end

**Odisha** faces cyclones (the 1999 super cyclone, Phailin 2013, Fani 2019), floods of the Mahanadi delta, and droughts in its western districts. It is now held up as a model: its early evacuation of over a million people cut cyclone deaths sharply.

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 2018
Which of the following statements are true?
1. Natural disasters cause maximum damage in developing countries.
2. Bhopal gas tragedy was man-made.
3. India is a disaster free country.
4. Mangroves reduce the impact of cyclones.
Select the correct answer from the codes given below:
(a) 1, 2 and 3
(b) 2, 3 and 4
(c) 1, 2 and 4
(d) 1, 3 and 4
@ans (c)
@end

The **Bhopal gas tragedy** (night of **2-3 December 1984**) was an industrial disaster: **methyl isocyanate** leaked from the Union Carbide pesticide plant. **Mangroves** absorb wave energy and storm surges, which is why they are now planted along cyclone-prone coasts.

@section 10.4 | Disaster Management Institutions

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2004
The first Disaster Management Training Institution of the country is being established at:
(a) Hyderabad ( Andhra Pradesh)
(b) Bangalore (Karnataka)
(c) Latur (Maharashtra)
(d) Chennai (Tamil Nadu)
@ans (c)
@end

After the **Latur earthquake of 1993**, the first disaster management training institute was planned at **Latur**. Nationally, the **National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM)**, New Delhi, handles training and research.

@table
Body | Set up | Role
NDMA | 2005 (DM Act) | Policy and plans; chaired by the Prime Minister
NDRF | 2006 | Specialist response battalions (16 battalions)
NIDM | 1995; statutory 2005 | Training and research, New Delhi
INCOIS (ITEWC) | 2007 | Tsunami and ocean-state warnings, Hyderabad
IMD | 1875 | Weather, cyclone and heat-wave warnings, New Delhi
SDMA, DDMA | 2005 | State and district authorities
@end

UP link: Uttar Pradesh treats **lightning**, **heat waves**, **cold waves** and **boat capsizing** as state-specific disasters, eligible for relief from the State Disaster Response Fund. Lightning is among the biggest killers in UP every monsoon.
