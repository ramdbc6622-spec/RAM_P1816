@chapter 5 | Tribes of India

@intro
India's Scheduled Tribes numbered **10.43 crore** in Census 2011, about **8.6%** of the population, in more than 700 notified groups. The **Bhil** are the largest, followed by the **Gond** and the **Santhal**. Madhya Pradesh has the largest tribal population. Mizoram and Lakshadweep have the highest share. UPPSC tests tribes almost entirely by matching: tribe with state or hill range, tribe with a custom (polyandry, transhumance, jhum, festivals) and tribe with racial group. The **Tharu** of the UP Tarai appear more than any other tribe. Several pairs in this chapter carry disputed keys, which are noted. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 5.1 | Racial Groups and the Basis of Tribal Identity

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2003
Which one of the following racial groups is found in hilly and forested areas of northeastern India:
(a) Dinarics
(b) Mediterranean
(c) Mongoloids
(d) Proto-Australoids
@ans (c)
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2002
Which one of the following tribes of India belongs to the Proto-Australoid race:
(a) Irula
(b) Khasi
(c) Santhal
(d) Tharu
@ans (c)
@end

B.S. Guha's classification (1937) is the one used in these questions. The **Mongoloid** strain is found in the north-eastern hills and the Himalayan belt. The **Proto-Australoid** strain is found among the central and eastern tribes (Santhal, Munda, Ho, Oraon, Bhil). The **Negrito** strain is found among the Andaman tribes.

@table
Racial group (B.S. Guha) | Typical tribes and regions
Negrito | Great Andamanese, Onge, Jarawa, Sentinelese; traces among the Kadar and Irula of the south
Proto-Australoid | Santhal, Munda, Ho, Oraon, Bhil, Kol (central and eastern India)
Mongoloid (Palaeo- and Tibeto-) | Naga, Khasi, Garo, Mizo, Lepcha, Bhutia (north-east and Himalaya)
Mediterranean | Main element of the Dravidian-speaking south and the plains
Western Brachycephals (incl. Dinaric) | Western India (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Coorg)
Nordic | North-west (Punjab, Rajasthan uplands)
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1992
What is the basis of the determination of Tribes in India?
(a) Cultural specification and different habitation.
(b) Language and dialect
(c) Differences in social customs
(d) Economic Status
@ans (a)
@end

The **Lokur Committee (1965)** listed five tests for a Scheduled Tribe: **primitive traits, distinctive culture, geographical isolation, shyness of contact with the wider community, and backwardness**. Under **Article 342** the President notifies the list for each state; Parliament can change it by law.

@section 5.2 | The Tharu and the Tribes of Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand

@pyq U.P. R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) 2017; U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1991
"Tharu Tribe" is residing in which of the following states of India?
(a) Bihar and Madhya Pradesh
(b) Jharkhand and Bihar
(c) Chhattisgarh and Himachal Pradesh
(d) Uttarakhand and Uttar Pradesh
@ans (d)
@end

The **Tharu** live in the **Tarai** along the Nepal border, in Uttarakhand (Udham Singh Nagar), Uttar Pradesh and Bihar (West Champaran), and across the border in Nepal. Census 2011 counted about 1.05 lakh in UP, 91,000 in Uttarakhand and 1.6 lakh in Bihar. The two-state option (d) is the intended answer.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2005; U.P. P.C.S. (Mains) 2015
Diwali is a festival of mourning among the –
(a) Khasis
(b) Mundas
(c) Bhils
(d) Tharus
@ans (d)
@end

@one
Which of the Hindu festivals is celebrated by the Tharu people as a festival of mourning
@ans Diwali
@end

UP link: Tharu villages lie in **Lakhimpur Kheri, Bahraich, Shravasti, Balrampur and Maharajganj**. Many Tharu groups claim descent from Rajput women who fled to the Tarai, and they are known for strong women's authority in the household. Tharu women's handicraft is promoted under UP's **One District One Product** scheme in Lakhimpur Kheri.

@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1997
Transhumance is practised by the :
(a) Bhotias
(b) Bhoksas
(c) Jaunsaris
(d) Tharus
@ans (a)
@end

**Transhumance** is the seasonal movement of people and herds between high summer pastures and lower winter grounds. The **Bhotia** of Uttarakhand move up to the high valleys (Niti, Mana, Milam, Darma) in summer and come down in winter. The **Gaddi** of Himachal and the **Gujjar-Bakarwal** of Jammu and Kashmir do the same.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Spl) (Mains) 2004
Which one of the following is not correctly matched ?
(a) Bhotia - Uttar Pradesh
(b) Khasi - Meghalaya
(c) Santhal - Jharkhand
(d) Toda - Tamil Nadu
@ans (*)
@note none of the pairs is wrong. The **Bhotia** are a notified ST in Uttar Pradesh too (5,196 in Census 2011), though most now live in Uttarakhand. The question was probably meant to make (a) the answer after Uttarakhand was formed in 2000.
@end

@pyq U.P.P.S.C. (GIC) 2010
Which one of the following pairs is not correctly matched?
(a) Angami – Nagaland
(b) Apatani – Arunachal Pradesh
(c) Bhotiya – Uttar Pradesh
(d) Gonds – Madhya Pradesh
@ans (*)
@note all four pairs are correct. **Bhotiya** is a notified ST of Uttar Pradesh and Uttarakhand. Do not confuse it with **Bhutia**, the ST of Sikkim, Tripura and West Bengal; with "Bhutia" in option (c), the answer would have been (c).
@end

@trap
**Bhotia/Bhotiya** (Uttarakhand, UP) and **Bhutia** (Sikkim) are different notified tribes. UPPSC's pairs with "Bhotia - Uttar Pradesh" have no wrong option, which is why both keys are marked (*). In Uttarakhand, the largest ST is **Tharu**, just ahead of Jaunsari.
@end

@section 5.3 | Customs: Polyandry, Jhum and Festivals

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2005
Which of the following tribes practise polyandry?
(a) Kadar
(b) Lodha
(c) Munda
(d) Toda
@ans (d)
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2009 ; U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2006
Which of the following tribes of India practice polyandry? Select the answer from the code given below :
1. Gonds
2. Nagas
3. Jaunsaris
4. Todas
Code :
(a) 1 and 2
(b) 2 and 3
(c) 3 and 4
(d) 1 and 4
@ans (c)
@end

**Fraternal polyandry** (one woman married to brothers) was recorded among the **Toda** and **Kota** of the Nilgiris, the **Jaunsari** (Khasa) of Jaunsar-Bawar in Uttarakhand, and some Himalayan groups of Kinnaur and Ladakh. It is now rare. The Gond and Naga are not polyandrous.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S (Pre) 2010
Jhooming is practiced by –
(a) The Bhotiyas
(b) The Khasis
(c) The Santhals
(d) The Todas
@ans (b)
@end

**Jhum** is the north-eastern name for **slash-and-burn shifting cultivation**, practised by the Khasi, Garo, Naga, Mizo and other hill tribes. It has many regional names.

@table
Name of shifting cultivation | Region
Jhum | North-east (Assam, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Mizoram)
Podu | Andhra Pradesh, Telangana
Bewar, Dahiya | Madhya Pradesh (Baiga)
Penda, Dippa | Chhattisgarh (Bastar)
Kumari | Western Ghats (Kerala, Karnataka)
Valre, Waltre | Rajasthan
Kurwa | Jharkhand
Milpa | Mexico and Central America
Ladang | Indonesia, Malaysia
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2005
One of the tribes which celebrates 'Sarhul' festival is –
(a) Santhal
(b) Munda
(c) Bhil
(d) Tharu
@ans (*)
@note both (a) and (b) are correct. **Sarhul**, the spring festival of the sal blossom (in Chaitra), is celebrated by the **Oraon, Munda, Ho and Santhal** of Jharkhand.
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2016
Which tribe worships 'Lohasur' as their deity ?
(a) Gond
(b) Bhil
(c) Korku
(d) Agaria
@ans (d)
@end

The **Agaria** are traditional iron-smelters of the Maikal hills (Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh). **Lohasur**, the iron demon, lives in their furnace and is their chief deity.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2005
Which one of the following is not correctly matched?
(a) Bihu – Assam
(b) Onam – Andhra Pradesh
(c) Pongal – Tamil Nadu
(d) Baisakhi – Punjab
@ans (b)
@end

**Onam** is the harvest festival of **Kerala**. It celebrates the return of King Mahabali, with boat races and floral carpets (pookalam).

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2006
Which one of the following pairs is not correctly matched?
State - Main Language
(a) Goa - Konkani
(b) Meghalaya - Khasi
(c) Nagaland - Angami
(d) Sikkim - Tibetan
@ans (d)
@end

The main languages of **Sikkim** are **Nepali**, **Bhutia (Sikkimese)** and **Lepcha**. Nepali is in the Eighth Schedule; Tibetan is not a state language.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2002
'Khasi and Garo' Language speaking population is found in :
(a) Madhya Pradesh
(b) Meghalaya
(c) Tripura
(d) Assam
@ans (b) & (d)
@end

Khasi and Garo are spoken mainly in **Meghalaya**, with smaller groups across the border in **Assam**. Both are associate official languages of Meghalaya; English is the official one.

@next
**PVTGs** (Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups, 75 in number) are in the news through the **PM-JANMAN** mission (2023). Expect a question asking which of the Andaman tribes, the Chenchu, the Baiga or the Raji are PVTGs: all of them are.
@end

@heatmap

@pattern
Matching dominates: about half of this chapter is tribe-state pairs. Three habits pay off: fix one or two anchors per code (Toda, Lepcha, Birhor), keep **Bhotia** (Uttarakhand) apart from **Bhutia** (Sikkim), and remember the Tharu customs. UPPSC keys in this chapter are less reliable than elsewhere: five items carry (*) or dual answers. In the exam, choose the option the key intends (usually the one with the obvious mismatch) and do not over-think spelling variants.
@end
