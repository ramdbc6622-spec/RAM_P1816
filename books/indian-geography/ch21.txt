@chapter 21 | Mineral Resources II: Coal, Petroleum and Natural Gas

@intro
**Coal** is India's chief source of commercial energy and still generates about **70%** of its electricity. Nearly all of it lies in **Gondwana** basins along the **Damodar, Son, Mahanadi and Godavari** valleys; the young **Tertiary** coals of the north-east and the **lignite** of Neyveli and Gujarat are minor. India produced over **one billion tonnes** of coal in 2024-25, second only to China. **Petroleum** comes from Tertiary sedimentary basins: **Mumbai High** offshore, **Gujarat** and **Rajasthan** onshore, and **Assam**, where India's oil story began. This chapter covers coal reserves and production, coalfields, lignite, oilfields, refineries, natural gas and pipelines. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@one U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2017; U.P. R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) (Re-Exam) 2016
Which one rock systems provides over 90% of the coal of India?
@ans Gondwana system

About **99.5%** of India's coal resources are in **Lower Gondwana** rocks (about 250 million years old). The rest are **Tertiary** coals (about 50 million years) of Assam, Meghalaya and Arunachal Pradesh, which are high in sulphur.
