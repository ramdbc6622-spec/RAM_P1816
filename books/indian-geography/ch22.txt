@chapter 22 | Power and Energy Resources

@intro
India is the world's third largest producer and consumer of electricity. At the end of 2025 its installed capacity was about **514 GW**. **Coal and lignite** remain the largest single source (about 226 GW), but in **June 2025** non-fossil sources (solar, wind, hydro, nuclear, bio-power) crossed **half** of installed capacity, five years ahead of the 2030 target. Solar (about 136 GW) is now second to coal, followed by wind (about 55 GW), large hydro (about 51 GW) and nuclear (about 8.8 GW). This chapter covers thermal power and its stations, nuclear power, hydro and other renewables, tidal and geothermal energy. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 22.3 | Nuclear Power

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2005; U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2002; U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2003

@pyq U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2006
Consider the following statements:
Assertion (A): Nuclear energy is a promising source of futuristic demand for energy supply in India.
Reason (R) : Nuclear minerals are ubiquitously available in India.
Select correct answer by using codes given below:
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
@ans (c)
@end

India has little good-grade **uranium** but about a quarter of the world's **thorium** (in monazite sands). Homi Bhabha's **three-stage programme** uses natural uranium in pressurised heavy water reactors, then plutonium in **fast breeder reactors** (the 500 MW PFBR at Kalpakkam), and finally thorium. Uranium is imported from Kazakhstan, Canada, Russia and others since the 2008 India-US civil nuclear deal.

@pyq
India's first nuclear reactor was –
(a) Apsara
(b) Kamini
(c) Rohini
(d) Urvashi
@ans (a)

**Apsara** at Trombay went critical in **August 1956**, the first research reactor in Asia. **Kamini** (Kalpakkam, 1996) is the only reactor in the world using uranium-233. The first nuclear **power** station was **Tarapur** (Maharashtra, **1969**), built with US help.

@heatmap

@pattern
Energy questions have two layers. The older papers ask for **station-state-type** pairs: thermal (Singrauli, Obra, Korba, Ramagundam, Patratu), nuclear (Tarapur, Rawatbhata, Narora, Kakrapar, Kaiga, Kudankulam), wind (Muppandal) and tidal (Khambhat). The newer ones test the **energy mix**, where the data has moved fast: non-fossil capacity passed 50% in 2025, solar is second to coal, and Gujarat leads in wind. UP has a strong presence: Singrauli (NTPC's first plant), Obra (Soviet-aided), Narora (atomic), Rampura (solar village) and UP's top rank in thermal capacity.
@end
