@chapter 2 | Physical Divisions: Structure, Mountains, Peaks, Passes and Glaciers

@intro
India's relief has three old-to-young layers. The **Peninsular Plateau** is a fragment of Gondwanaland and is the oldest and most stable block. The **Himalaya** is a young fold system, raised by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates and still rising. The **Northern Plains** between them are the youngest, built from Himalayan alluvium. Coastal plains and islands (Chapter 3) complete the six physiographic divisions. UPPSC asks this chapter mostly as location and matching: which hills lie in which state, which pass leads where, which peak or glacier is largest. This chapter covers geology and rock systems, the Himalaya and its ranges, the plains, the old mountains of the Peninsula, peaks, passes, glaciers and plateaus. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 2.1 | Geological History and Rock Systems

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1998
The paleomagnetic results obtained from India indicate that in the past, the Indian landmass has moved :
(a) Northward
(b) Southward
(c) Eastward
(d) Westward
@ans (a)
@end

Iron minerals in old lava record the direction of the Earth's magnetic field when they cool. Rocks of the **Deccan Traps** record a position far south of the Equator, which shows that the **Indian plate drifted north** after leaving **Gondwanaland** (with Africa, South America, Antarctica and Australia). It moved fast, around 15 cm a year at its peak, and still moves north by about 5 cm a year.

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2004
Which of the following statements is incorrect ?
(a) Geologically Peninsular region is India's most ancient part.
(b) Himalaya mountains demonstrate the most newly developed folded mountains in the world.
(c) The Western coastline of India is formed by the deposition process of the rivers.
(d) Gondwana rocks have the largest reserve of coal in India.
@ans (c)
@end

The **west coast** was shaped by **faulting and partial submergence** of the plateau edge, not by river deposition. Its rivers (Narmada, Tapi) end in **estuaries**, not deltas. The east coast, by contrast, is a wide plain built by the deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri. About **98% of India's coal** is in **Gondwana** rocks of the Damodar, Mahanadi, Son and Godavari valleys.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2024
Which one of the following pairs (Deccan Trap Peculiarity) is not correctly matched?
(a) Depth of middle trap – 1200 metres approximately
(b) Intertrappean beds – Fossils of sea plants and animals
(c) Depth of lower trap – 150 metres approximately
(d) Depth of upper trap – 450 metres approximately
@ans (b)
@end

The **Deccan Traps** are layers of basalt from fissure eruptions at the end of the Cretaceous. Between flows, lakes and swamps left thin sedimentary **intertrappean beds**. Their fossils are of **freshwater and land** plants and animals, not marine life, which shows that the flows were poured out on land. The trap is divided into upper (about 450 m), middle (about 1,200 m) and lower (about 150 m) parts.

@trap
Latest of the four is **Gondwana**. Do not pick Vindhyan because the Vindhya range looks "younger" than the Dharwars: all three of Dharwar, Cuddapah and Vindhyan are Precambrian. In the Deccan Trap pair, "sea fossils" is the error.
@end

@section 2.2 | The Himalaya: Origin, Age and Structure

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2024
Given below are two statements, one is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other as Reason (R).
Assertion (A): The Himalayas are still rising.
Reason (R): The Indian tectonic plate is colliding with the Eurasian plate.
Select the correct answer from the codes given below:
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(b) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
(c) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(d) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
@ans (c)
@end

The Indian plate began to collide with Eurasia about **40 to 50 million years ago** and closed the **Tethys Sea**. Its sediments were folded into the Himalaya. The plate is still pushing north, so the range is still rising by a few millimetres a year, and the collision is also why the region is seismically active.

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 2019
Given below are two statements, one is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other as Reason (R).
Assertion (A) : The highest concentration of seismic zones lies in Himalayan region in India.
Reason (R) : There are many longitudinal thrust zones in Himalayas.
Codes :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A)
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A)
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true
@ans (a)
@end

The Himalaya is cut by long thrust faults that run parallel to the range: the **Main Central Thrust**, the **Main Boundary Thrust** and the **Himalayan Frontal Thrust**. Stress released along them causes earthquakes, so most of the Himalaya and the north-east fall in **Seismic Zones IV and V**. In the Bureau of Indian Standards' 2025 seismic map, the whole Himalayan arc is placed in the highest zone.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1994
The south of 'Shiwalik' rock series, Bhabar region is an example of -
(a) Midland Region
(b) Intermountain Region
(c) Piedmont Region
(d) Littoral Region
@ans (c)
@end

Rivers leaving the Shiwaliks drop their pebbles and boulders at the foot of the hills. This **piedmont** belt, 8 to 16 km wide, is the **Bhabar**. Streams sink into it and flow underground. They reappear further south in the marshy, forested **Tarai**. Beyond lie the old alluvial terraces of **Bhangar** and the new, yearly flooded **Khadar**.

Bhangar | Older alluvium, above flood level, kankar nodules | Most of the doab uplands
Khadar | New alluvium, renewed by floods every year | River floodplains of the Ganga and Yamuna
@end

UP link: the Tarai of **Pilibhit, Lakhimpur Kheri and Bahraich** holds UP's best forests and its tiger reserves (Dudhwa, Pilibhit). Much of the old Tarai was cleared for farming after Independence.

@trap
Both Bhabar and Tarai are "along the sub-Himalayan region". UPPSC's key picks **Bhabar** because it is the belt **at the foot** of the Shiwalik. Tarai is the next belt south. A **doon** is a valley inside the hills, not a plain along them.
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2005
Which of the following is an example of 'Residual Mountain' ?
(a) Himalaya
(b) Kilimanjaro
(c) Etna
(d) Aravalli
@ans (d)
@end

The **Aravalli** is among the oldest fold mountains of the world, formed in the Precambrian. It runs about 800 km from **Palanpur (Gujarat)** through **Rajasthan** and **Haryana** to the **Delhi Ridge**. Long erosion has worn it to stumps, which makes it a **residual (relict) mountain**. Its highest point is **Guru Shikhar (1,722 m)** on Mount Abu. Kilimanjaro and Etna are volcanic mountains, and the Himalaya is a young fold mountain.

@pattern
This is the largest chapter in the book after agriculture and minerals. UPPSC tests it almost entirely through **location**: which pass in which state, which hills in which state, which peak is highest, which glacier is longest. The pass items alone fill one fifth of the chapter, and the "state and its pass" pairing has been asked in nearly every decade. The 2019 to 2025 papers added new formats: Assertion-Reason on Himalayan uplift and seismic thrusts, the Atal Tunnel, the Shaksgam Valley, Deccan Trap thicknesses and the "state and highest peak" match. Learn one master table each for passes, peaks, hills and glaciers. Remember that Lipulekh left UP in 2000 and that Zoji La and Chang La are now in Ladakh.
