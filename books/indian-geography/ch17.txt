@chapter 17 | Major Crops II: Cotton, Sugarcane, Oilseeds and Pulses

@intro
After the cereals, UPPSC turns to the cash and protein crops. **Sugarcane** is the crop of Uttar Pradesh: UP grows about half of India's cane and is called the **"sugar bowl"**, though the industry has been shifting south for better yields and longer crushing. **Cotton** is the crop of the black-soil Deccan. **Oilseeds** (soybean, groundnut, mustard) are led by Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat. India is the world's largest producer, consumer and importer of **pulses**, led by Madhya Pradesh. This chapter covers these crops with their states, varieties and growing facts. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2009
Cotton fibres are obtained from –
(a) Leaves
(b) Seed
(c) Stem
(d) Root
@ans (b)
@end

Cotton fibre (**lint**) is a hair that grows from the **seed coat** inside the boll; jute and flax fibres, by contrast, come from the **stem**. Cotton needs 210 frost-free days, 50-100 cm of rain and the moisture-holding **black soil** of the Deccan. **Gujarat** and **Maharashtra** are the largest producers; **Bt cotton** (since 2002) covers over 90% of the area.

@pyq U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2013
Which of the following crops has the largest percentage of irrigated area of its net sown area in India?
(a) Wheat
(b) Rice
(c) Oilseed
(d) Sugarcane

About **96%** of the sugarcane area is irrigated, slightly ahead of wheat (about 95%) and far ahead of rice (about 65%) and oilseeds (under 35%). Sugarcane stays in the field for 10 to 18 months and needs water through the dry season.

@one U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A (Spl.) (Mains), 2010
Sugarcane seeds are produced in
@ans SBI, Coimbatore
@end

The **Sugarcane Breeding Institute, Coimbatore** (1912) is the only place in India where cane flowers naturally and sets true seed, so all national breeding (the "Co" varieties, such as Co 0238) is done there. **Lucknow** has the **Indian Institute of Sugarcane Research** (1952), and **Shahjahanpur** has the **UP Council of Sugarcane Research** (1912). Both do agronomy and testing, not seed breeding.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2007
A large quantity of gypsum is required for –
(a) Rice cultivation
(b) Barseem cultivation
(c) Wheat cultivation
(d) Groundnut cultivation
@ans (d)
@end

After a **groundnut** flower is fertilised, its stalk (the **peg**) grows down and pushes the young pod into the soil, where it ripens. Loose, light soil helps pegging. **Gypsum** (calcium sulphate) applied at flowering supplies the **calcium** the pods need and **sulphur** for oil, so groundnut is the crop that needs it most.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Spl) (Mains) 2004
Which nutrient is considered essential for growing pulse crop ?
(a) Chromium
(b) Cobalt
(c) Iodine
(d) Sodium
@ans (b)
@end

**Cobalt** is needed by the *Rhizobium* bacteria in legume root nodules: it forms part of vitamin B12, which the bacteria need to fix nitrogen. **Molybdenum** is the other key micronutrient for nodulation.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2009
Pulse crop which does not fix atmospheric nitrogen is
(a) Gram
(b) Pea
(c) Rajma
(d) Moong
@ans (*)
@note the intended answer is (c). **Rajma** (*Phaseolus vulgaris*) forms few effective nodules in Indian soils and needs nitrogen fertiliser, so textbooks describe it as a poor fixer. Strictly, it is a legume and does fix nitrogen with the right *Rhizobium* strain, which is why the key is marked (*).
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2013
Balanced Fertilizers Ratio (NPK) for legume crops is –
(a) 1 : 2 : 2
(b) 3 : 2 : 1
(c) 4 : 2 : 1
(d) 2 : 2 : 1
@ans (a)
@end

Legumes make their own nitrogen, so they need only a small **starter dose of N** and more **phosphorus and potash**: a ratio of about **1:2:2**. Cereals need the general ratio of about 4:2:1.
