@chapter 11 | Soils

@intro
The **Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR)** recognises eight major soil groups: **alluvial, black (regur), red and yellow, laterite, arid (desert), saline and alkaline, peaty and marshy, and forest and mountain** soils. **Alluvial** soils cover the largest area (about two-fifths of India) and are the most productive. Black soils come second in agricultural value, then red soils. UPPSC asks about soils through their parent rock, colour, crops and problems, especially **usar** (alkaline) land and ravine erosion, both important in Uttar Pradesh. This chapter covers the main soil groups, nutrients, saline and alkaline soils, and soil erosion. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@one U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2013; U.P.P.C.S. (Spl) (Mains) 2004

**Black soils** come from the weathering of **Deccan basalt**. Their colour comes mainly from **titaniferous magnetite** and iron compounds with clay, not from humus. They are rich in **lime, iron, magnesia and potash** but poor in **nitrogen, phosphorus and organic matter**. Their clay holds water for long, which suits **cotton** and is why they are called "black cotton soil".

Two statements are given below, one is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other as Reason (R):
Assertion (A): The red colour of red soil is due to the wide diffusion of iron rather than its high proportion.

**Red soils** form on old **granite and gneiss** in areas of low rainfall. They ring the black-soil region on the south, east and north (Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Telangana, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand). Iron oxide coats the particles thinly but widely, giving the red colour; where the iron is hydrated, the soil is **yellow**. Lack of nitrogen and humus is true, but it has nothing to do with colour.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S.(Pre) 2001
Which one of the following statements is not true for Laterite soils:
(a) They are the soils of the humid tropical regions.
(b) They are highly leached soils
(c) Their fertility is low
(d) They are rich in lime
@ans (d)
@end

**Laterite** (Latin *later*, brick) forms where heavy rain alternates with a dry season and high heat. Rain washes out **silica, lime and bases** (leaching), and **iron and aluminium oxides** are left behind. The soil is poor in nitrogen, potash, lime and humus. It hardens on exposure and is cut into building bricks. With manure it supports **tea, coffee, cashew and rubber**.

UP link: almost all of UP is **alluvial**. Local names: **bhangar** (old, higher alluvium with kankar), **khadar** (new floodplain alluvium), **bhur** (sandy soil of western UP river margins), and **matiyar** and **dumat** (clay and loam) in farmers' usage.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2002

Tea needs **acidic** (pH 4.5 to 5.5), deep, well-drained soil rich in humus. That is why it grows on hill slopes of **Assam, Darjeeling and the Nilgiris** with heavy, well-spread rain.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Spl) (Mains) 2004
The micronutrient maximum deficient in Indian soils, is :
(a) Copper
(b) Iron
(c) Manganese

@ans (d)

**Zinc** is the most widely deficient micronutrient in Indian soils (over a third of soil samples tested), followed by boron and sulphur. **Khaira disease** of rice is caused by zinc deficiency. The **Soil Health Card scheme** (2015) tests soil and advises nutrient doses.

@section 11.3 | Saline and Alkaline (Usar) Soils

@pyq U.P. B.E.O. (Pre) 2019
Following are the statements regarding the Usar soil:
1. It is reclaimed by adding lime.
2. This soil has pH more than seven.
3. Paddy crop can be grown in this soil.
Select the correct answer from the codes given below:
Codes :
(a) 1 and 2 are correct
(b) 2 and 3 are correct
(c) only 3 is correct
(d) only 1 is correct
@ans (b)
@end

**Usar** (also *reh* or *kallar*) is **alkaline (sodic)** land with pH often above 8.5 and a white salt crust. It is reclaimed with **gypsum** or **pyrite**, plus green manure of **dhaincha**. Lime is used for **acidic** soils, not alkaline ones. Salt-tolerant rice varieties (such as CSR 30, CSR 36) are the first crop after reclamation.

UP link: **Uttar Pradesh** has India's largest **alkaline (sodic)** area, mostly in the central Ganga plain: Rae Bareli, Unnao, Lucknow, Kanpur, Sultanpur, Pratapgarh, Mainpuri, Etah and Aligarh. The **Central Soil Salinity Research Institute** has a regional station at **Lucknow**, and the UP Bhumi Sudhar Nigam has reclaimed lakhs of hectares since the 1990s.

@section 11.4 | Soil Erosion and Conservation

@pyq U.P. R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) 2023
Consider the following types of erosion and arrange these in correct sequence in terms of the increasing order of soil loss from the field due to these types of erosion :
I. Gully erosion
II. Splash erosion
III. Rill erosion
IV. Sheet erosion
Select the correct answer from the code given below :
Code :
(a) II, III, IV, I
(b) IV, I, III, II
(c) II, IV, III, I
(d) III, II, I, IV
@ans (c)
@end

Raindrops first dislodge particles (**splash**). Run-off then strips a thin uniform layer (**sheet**), cuts small channels (**rills**) and finally deep channels (**gullies**). Gullies that merge produce **ravines** (badlands).

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2004

(a) Malwa Plateau
(b) Foothill areas of Shivalik ranges
(c) Dandkaranya
(d) Meghalaya Plateau
@ans (b)
@end

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2004
Which one of the following areas is most affected by soil erosion in India?
(a) Malwa Plateau
(b) Tarai area of Uttar Pradesh
(c) Coastal area of Andhra
(d) Chambal valley
@ans (d)
@end

The **Shiwalik foothills** of Punjab and Himachal (where seasonal torrents called **choes** cut the slopes, as in Hoshiarpur) and the **Chambal ravines** are India's worst erosion zones. The Chambal, Yamuna and their tributaries have cut gullies up to 30 m deep across Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh.

UP link: UP's **ravine lands** lie along the Yamuna and Chambal in **Agra, Etawah, Auraiya, Jalaun and Hamirpur**. They are being reclaimed by afforestation, check dams and gully plugging.

@heatmap

@pattern
Soils are asked through a short list of facts: regur comes from basalt and suits cotton, laterite is leached and lacks lime, alluvial soil is the most productive, and tea needs acid soil. Recent papers (2023, 2024) favour **Assertion-Reason** on red, laterite and saline soils and a sequencing item on erosion. Uttar Pradesh brings two of its own topics: **usar** reclamation (gypsum, pH above 7, rice) and **ravine** erosion in the Chambal-Yamuna belt. Keep saline (Gujarat) and alkaline (UP) apart.
