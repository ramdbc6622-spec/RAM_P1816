@chapter 27 | The High Court and Subordinate Courts
@meta Built from 13 UPPSC questions (1997–2016). Focus: HC judges' appointment and pension, number of High Courts, March 2013 courts, writs, Art 226 vs Art 32.

@hot
- **Acting and additional judges** exist only in **High Courts** (Art 224) {2016}
- Retired HC judge's **pension**: **Consolidated Fund of India** (salary from the State fund) {2013}
- New High Courts in **March 2013**: **Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura** {2005, 2013}
- **Certiorari** = transfer/quash a lower court's record; **Prohibition** = case pending {2008, 2008, 2010}
- **Art 226** is wider than **Art 32** (covers legal rights too) {1997, 2004}
@end

@section 27.1 | Appointment and Tenure
- Not correct: the **Governor** appoints HC judges (the **President** does) {2016}
- Acting judges: **High Court** {2016}

@section 27.2 | Salary and Pension
- HC judge's pension: **Consolidated Fund of India** {2013}

@section 27.3 | Number and Benches
- High Courts: 21 in 2008; **25** now (*) {2008}
- No own HC in 2005: **Manipur** (got one in 2013) (*) {2005}
- Established March 2013: **Meghalaya and Tripura** (and Manipur) {2013}
- Most benches: **Bombay** now; Gauhati had six in 2007 {2007}

@table
High Court | Covers
Gauhati | Assam, Nagaland, Mizoram, Arunachal
Bombay | Maharashtra, Goa, Dadra-Nagar Haveli and Daman-Diu
Calcutta | West Bengal, Andaman and Nicobar
Kerala | Kerala, Lakshadweep
Madras | Tamil Nadu, Puducherry
Punjab and Haryana | Punjab, Haryana, Chandigarh
J&K and Ladakh | Both UTs
@end

@section 27.4 | Jurisdiction
- Not a main HC jurisdiction: **advisory** {2007}

@section 27.5 | Writs
- Certiorari: superior court calls up the **record** from an inferior court {2008}
- Examines subordinate courts' work: **certiorari** {2008}
- While a case is **pending**: **prohibition** {2010}

@flow Which writ, when
Unlawful detention | Habeas corpus
Duty not done | Mandamus
Case pending below | Prohibition
Order already passed | Certiorari
Office held illegally | Quo warranto
@end

@section 27.6 | Art 226 vs Art 32
- HC writ power covers FRs, legal rights and more (all) {1997}
- HCs better placed: SC's Art 32 covers only FRs (R explains A) {2004}

@section 27.7 | Subordinate Courts
- District judges appointed by the **Governor** in consultation with the HC (Art 233); HC controls them (Art 235)

@trap
- **Governor** gives the HC judge's **oath** (Art 219), but the **President** appoints.
- Salary: **State** fund; pension: **Union** fund.
- Gauhati HC still serves **four** States.
@end

@up The **Allahabad High Court** (est. Agra 1866, Allahabad 1869) is India's largest HC by sanctioned strength (160), with a **Lucknow Bench** (1948). UP district judges are appointed by the **UP Governor** with the HC.

@last
- President appoints HC judges; Governor gives oath
- Acting and additional judges = **HC only** (Art 224)
- Retirement **62**; resign to President
- Pension from **Consolidated Fund of India**
- 25 High Courts; **Manipur, Meghalaya, Tripura** in 2013
- Gauhati HC = 4 States
- Certiorari = record; Prohibition = pending
- **Art 226** wider than Art 32
- No advisory jurisdiction for HCs
- District judges: **Governor** + HC (233)
@end
