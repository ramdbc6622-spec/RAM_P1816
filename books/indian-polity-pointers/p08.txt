@chapter 8 | The Governance System
@meta Built from 40 UPPSC questions (1990–2025). Focus: parliamentary vs presidential, features of the polity, republic, quasi-federalism and the scholars behind each label.

@hot
- India = **parliamentary** system; executive responsible to legislature; not presidential {1990, 2005, 2008, 2010, 2015, 2025}
- **Quasi-federal**: **K.C. Wheare** {2010, 2019, 2023, 2024}
- **Single citizenship** is **not** a federal feature {2015}
- Constitution = **partly rigid, partly flexible** {2005, 2012}
- **Republic** = elected head, no hereditary ruler {1997, 2017}
@end

@section 8.1 | Sovereignty
- Most prominent element of a state: **sovereignty** {2008}
- India is in the **Commonwealth**; its sovereignty is **not** curtailed {2017}

@section 8.2 | Parliamentary System
- India parliamentary; executive responsible to legislature (R explains A; option d in 2025) {2025}
- Constitution provides **parliamentary** governance {1990}
- In a parliamentary system the **legislature controls the executive** {2010}
- First in **Great Britain** {2018}
- Incorrect about India: **presidential system** {2008}; not a feature: **presidential government** {2015}
- Correct: India is a **parliamentary democracy** {2005}

@section 8.3 | Presidential System
- All executive power in the **President** {2005}
- Basic feature: **single executive** {2006}
- Main features: all of the listed options (key) {2014}

@table
Parliamentary (India, UK) | Presidential (USA)
Dual executive (nominal + real) | Single executive
CoM from legislature | Secretaries from outside
Collective responsibility to LS | Fixed term, no confidence vote
Lower house can be dissolved | No dissolution
@end

@section 8.4 | Cardinal Features
- True: **democratic republic, parliamentary form, sovereignty in the people**; false: unified authority {2008, 2009}
- India: democratic polity, sovereign state, democratic society, welfare state (all true) {2012}
- India democratic; has its own Constitution (both true, R not the explanation) {2017}
- Not a feature: **authoritarian government** {2011}

@section 8.5 | Supremacy and Republic
- Supreme: the **Constitution** {2013}; Centre and States get authority from it {2017}
- Republic: **no hereditary rulers** {1997}; head of State **elected** {2017}
- Not democratic: supreme power in one person like a king {2010}
- Constitution: **partly rigid, partly flexible** {2012}
- "Good balance between rigidity and flexibility": **K.C. Wheare** (per key) {2005}
- Bulkiest Constitution: all reasons valid (*) {1997}
- Not its philosophy: **communist state** {2015}

@section 8.6 | India vs UK and USA
- India differs from UK by **judicial review** {2010}
- Common to India and USA: **pocket veto** (only 3) {2017}

@section 8.7 | Federal and Quasi-Federal
- Not federal: **single citizenship** {2015}
- Federal system; strong Centre (both true, R not the explanation) {1995}
- "Federalism not practical" true per key; "India is not federal" false {1998}
- True: federal structure with **unitary bias**; false: both legislatures sovereign {2004}
- **Cooperative-Granville Austin, bargaining-Morris-Jones, quasi-federal-K.C. Wheare, centralising tendency-Ivor Jennings** {2024}
- "Quasi-federal": **K.C. Wheare** {2010, 2019, 2023}
- "Not set in a tight mould of federalism": **Ambedkar** {2008}
- Quasi-federal; independent judiciary (both true, R not the explanation) {2020}
- Quasi-federal because neither federal nor unitary (R explains A) {2007}
- Shift from centralism to federalism: **1960s** {2008}

@section 8.8 | Parties and Styles
- Parties are the life-blood of democracy (key: R explains A) {2009}
- Continuity and change: modern, traditional, saintly styles (Morris-Jones) (R explains A) {2020}

@trap
- **Single citizenship**, integrated judiciary, All India Services = **unitary** features.
- "Unified authority" and "both legislatures sovereign" are wrong statements.
- Common to India and USA: pocket veto; residuary is with Centre in India, States in USA.
@end
