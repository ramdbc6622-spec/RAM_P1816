@chapter 18 | The Attorney-General and the C.A.G.
@meta Built from 34 UPPSC questions (1991–2023). Focus: AG's pleasure tenure and rights in Parliament, Advocate General, CAG's tenure, audit role and the PAC.

@hot
- **Attorney General** may **speak in Parliament** but **cannot vote** (Art 88) {2003, 2005, 2011, 2017, 2023}
- AG: appointed by President, **SC-judge qualification**, holds office **at President's pleasure** {2014, 2016, 2020, 2023, 2023}
- AG = first law officer and chief legal adviser of the Government of India {1995, 2008, 2010}
- **CAG** (Art 148): **6 years**, removed like an SC judge, "guardian of the public purse" {1993, 2001, 2006, 2008, 2014, 2015}
- CAG is the **friend and guide of the PAC** {2005, 2006, 2011}
@end

@section 18.1 | Attorney General (Art 76)
- Holds office at the **President's pleasure** {2016}
- First law officer: **Attorney-General** {2010}; chief legal adviser {1995}; adviser to the **Government of India** {2008}
- Right of audience in all courts: **AG** {2015}
- Qualified as an SC judge (true); removed like SC judges (false) (only 1) {2023}
- SC-judge qualification (true); pay fixed by **Parliament** (false, President fixes it) (only 1) {2020}

@section 18.2 | AG and Parliament
- Appointed by President; SC-judge qualification (1, 2); not an MP, not impeached {2014}
- Right to address Parliament: **AG** {2011}
- Speaks and joins committees, **no vote**: AG {2003}
- Takes part in **both Houses**: AG {2005}
- Pleasure of the PM (false); no MP privileges (false): neither {2023}
- Not correct: AG **can vote** in Parliament {2017}

@section 18.3 | SG and Advocate General
- Solicitor General: a **legal adviser** {1991, 1994}
- State legal adviser: **Advocate General** (Art 165) {2014, 2015}

@table

Solicitor General | Statutory | Assists AG; not constitutional
Advocate General | 165 | HC-judge qualification; pleasure of Governor; Art 177

@end

@section 18.4 | CAG: Appointment and Tenure
- Appointed by the **President** {2001}; under **Art 148** {2015}
- Union audit reports go first to the **President** (Art 151) {2013}
- Office created **by the Constitution** {2012}
- Tenure: **6 years** (or 65) {1993}
- Not true: removable by the President at will {2014}
- Removal: **President's order after address by both Houses** (*) {2006}

@flow CAG removal (like an SC judge)
Motion | In either House
Special majority | Each House: majority of total + two-thirds present
Address | To the President
Order | President removes
@end

@section 18.5 | Audit, Not Control
- Not his duty: **controlling the issue of public money** {2004}
- "**Guardian of the public purse**": CAG {2008}
- Accounts separated from audit: **1976** {2004, 2019}
- State accounts are **not** controlled by any listed body (one CAG audits all) {2003}

@section 18.6 | CAG and the PAC
- Action on CAG remarks: **Parliament** {2014}
- Attends PAC meetings: **CAG** {2011}; CAG = friend and guide of the **PAC** {2006}
- **VP-Rajya Sabha, CAG-PAC, Solicitor General-Supreme Court, AG-Lok Sabha meetings** {2005}

@section 18.7 | Public Finance
- Not part of public finance: **functioning of commercial banks** {2014}

@trap
- AG pay fixed by the **President**; CAG pay by **Parliament**.
- AG serves at **pleasure**; CAG has **security of tenure**.
- AG speaks but **never votes**; not an MP.
- There is no "Advocate General of India" and no State CAG.
@end

@up UP's **Advocate General** sits at the **Allahabad High Court** (Prayagraj) and can speak in the UP Legislature (Art 177). CAG's UP reports go to the **Governor** of UP (Art 151(2)), then to the Vidhan Sabha PAC.
