@chapter 11 | Citizenship
@meta Built from 8 UPPSC questions (1994–2020). Focus: Part II, single citizenship, loss of citizenship, OCI, CAA 2019.

@hot
- Citizenship = **Part II, Articles 5-11**; **Parliament** lays down conditions (Art 11) {2010, 2013, 2018, 2020}
- India has **single citizenship** {1994, 2015}
@end

@section 11.1 | Constitutional Provisions
- Citizenship provisions: **Part II** {2010, 2018}; **Articles 5 to 11** {2020}
- Competent to prescribe conditions: **Parliament** {2013}

@section 11.2 | Single Citizenship
- Citizenship under the Constitution: **single** {1994}; single citizenship for all India {2015}

@section 11.3 | Loss of Citizenship
- Lost by **renunciation** and **voluntarily acquiring another citizenship**; not by marrying a foreigner or criticising the government {2017}

@flow Citizenship Act, 1955: losing citizenship
Renunciation | Voluntary declaration
Termination | Voluntarily takes another country's citizenship
Deprivation | By the Government (fraud, disloyalty, etc.)
@end

@section 11.4 | OCI
- Not eligible for OCI: an Indian who **migrated to Pakistan** (or Bangladesh) {2016}

@section 11.5 | CAA 2019
- Passed by Parliament: **11 December 2019** (RS); assent 12 December {2016}

@table
Item | CAA 2019
Countries | Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Pakistan
Communities | Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, Christian
Cut-off | Entered on or before 31 Dec 2014
Residence | 5 years (instead of 11) for naturalisation
Rules | Notified March 2024
@end

@trap
- **OCI is not dual citizenship**; OCI holders are foreign nationals.
- Marriage to a foreigner or criticism of the Government does **not** end citizenship.
- CAA passed 11 Dec 2019; **assent 12 Dec**.
@end

@up No UP-specific question. UP districts with Pakistani Hindu migrant settlements were among those authorised to receive CAA applications.
