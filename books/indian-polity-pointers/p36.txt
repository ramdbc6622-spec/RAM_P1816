@section 36.1 | Art 368 Procedure
- Parliament can amend any part under Art 368 (key: R explains A) {2015}
- Special majority in each House separately: **Constitution Amendment Bill** {2007}
- Provision: **Art 368** {2008}
- Initiated by **Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha** (1 and 2); in **either House** {2024, 2013, 2013}
- RS rejects an amendment Bill: the Bill **is dropped** (no joint sitting) {2004}

@flow Passing a Constitution Amendment Bill
Introduce | Either House; minister or private member
Each House | Majority of total + two-thirds present and voting
States (if federal) | Half the State legislatures ratify
President | Must assent (24th Amendment)
@end

@section 36.2 | Types and Ratification
- Half the States must ratify: **federal provisions, Supreme Court, Art 368 itself** (1, 2, 3); not new States {2004}

@table
Method | Examples
Simple majority (outside Art 368) | New States (Arts 2-4), citizenship, Rajya Sabha seats, quorum
Special majority | FRs, DPSP, most provisions
Special majority + half the States | President's election, SC and HCs, Seventh Schedule, Art 368, representation in Parliament
@end

@trap
- No **joint sitting** and no **veto** for Amendment Bills.
- Ratification (half the States) is different from Art 3 consultation.
- **25th** created Art 31C for 39(b), (c); **42nd** widened it.
- **35th** = associate state; **36th** = Sikkim full State.
@end

@up The **73rd and 74th** Amendments shaped UP's 3-tier Panchayats and 17 Municipal Corporations. The **Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain** case (from Allahabad HC) led to the 39th Amendment, partly struck down.
