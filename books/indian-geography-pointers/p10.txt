- Tsunami Warning Centre: **Hyderabad** (INCOIS) {2012}
- **IMD** headquarters: **New Delhi** (est. 1875) {2012}

@section 10.2 | Earthquakes and Seismic Zones
- **Karnataka plateau** is **not** a high-seismic zone {2006}
- India has **4 seismic zones** {2013}
- City-zone pairs: Bhuj V, Srinagar V, Chennai III, Hyderabad II (three pairs wrong, *) {2014}
- **Koyna** is becoming quake-prone because the dam sits on an **old fault** (reservoir-induced) {2001}

@flow Seismic zones, highest risk to lowest
Zone V (very high) | North-east, J&K, Himachal, Uttarakhand, Kutch, north Bihar, Andamans
Zone IV (high) | Delhi, Himalayan foothills, most of the Ganga plain and western UP
Zone III (moderate) | Kerala, Goa, Mumbai, Chennai, central India
Zone II (low) | Most of the Peninsula: Karnataka, Hyderabad
@end

@trap
- **Zone I no longer exists**; zone II is the lowest. A Peninsular city like Hyderabad is never zone IV or V.
@end

@section 10.3 | Landslides and Floods
- Himalayan **landslides** have increased because of **large-scale mining** (R explains A) {2003}
- Most flood-prone state: **Bihar** {2000}
- Most flood-hit part of UP: the **eastern** region {2012}
- State facing the most natural disasters among the options: **Odisha** {2002}
- True: damage is worst in **developing** countries; **Bhopal** gas tragedy was man-made; **mangroves** reduce cyclone damage (not "India is disaster-free") {2018}

@up **Eastern UP** (Gorakhpur, Bahraich, Basti, Kushinagar) floods most, from the **Ghaghara, Rapti and Gandak** rising in Nepal. Western UP and **Delhi-NCR** lie in seismic **zone IV**; the Tarai belt near Nepal is high-risk too.
