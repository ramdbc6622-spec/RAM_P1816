@chapter 9 | Climate and the Monsoon
@meta Built from 25 UPPSC questions (1993–2025). Focus: monsoon branches, winter rain, rainfall extremes, local storms.

@hot
- **Winter rain**: north-west India from **western disturbances**; **Tamil Nadu** from the **north-east monsoon** {1993, 2008, 2009, 2012, 2019, 2021, ?}
- **Leh** gets the **least rainfall** among the options {2008, 2009, 2010}
- India is a **monsoon** land; the **Himalaya** gives it climatic distinctiveness {1996, 1998}
- **Tamil Nadu** coast stays **dry** in the south-west monsoon (parallel to the Bay branch, rain shadow of the Arabian branch) {2023}
@end

@section 9.1 | Monsoon Land and the Himalaya
- India is **monsoonal** because of the **Himalaya** (A and R true, R explains A) {1996, 1998}
- Himalayan vegetation varies because **climate changes with altitude** {2025}
- **Southern** Himalayan slopes are **wetter and greener**; the claim that northern slopes are greener is false {2023}
- **Agra** (16°C) is warmer than **Darjeeling** (4°C) in January because temperature **falls with altitude** {2022}

@section 9.2 | Arrival and Branches of the Monsoon
- Monsoon reaches **Kerala** first on the mainland (Andaman and Nicobar earliest overall) {2006}
- The Indian monsoon comes from the **south-west winds** {2016}
- **Tamil Nadu** coast is **dry** in June-September (A and R true, R explains A) {2023}

@flow How the south-west monsoon spreads
Andaman and Nicobar | About 20 May
Kerala (onset) | About 1 June
Arabian Sea branch | Western Ghats: heavy rain; Deccan in rain shadow
Bay of Bengal branch | North-east hills: Mawsynram, Cherrapunji
Turned west by the Himalaya | Ganga plain: rain falls from east to west
Whole country | By about 8 July
@end

@section 9.3 | Rainfall
- Highest average annual rainfall among the options: **Sikkim** {2016}
- Humid places among the options: **Kochi and Tezpur** {2010}
- Least rainfall: **Leh** {2008, 2009, 2010}
- "200 mm average" had no correct group (probably meant 200 cm) (*) {1994}
- India is a "thirsty land" despite heavy rain for **all the reasons given** (seasonal, uneven, run-off) {1994}
- Cost-effective water strategy: **rainwater harvesting** {2011}
- Inland waterways are weak because rain falls in **only four months** (R explains A) {2008}

@table
Annual rainfall | Regions
Over 200 cm | West coast, north-east, Andaman and Nicobar
100-200 cm | East India, middle Ganga plain
50-100 cm | West UP, Punjab, Haryana, inner Deccan
Below 50 cm | West Rajasthan, Kutch, Ladakh
@end

@section 9.4 | Winter Weather
- Winter is **dry** for most of India; NE trade winds (source key: R true but not the explanation) {2023}
- Most **north-east monsoon** rain: **Tamil Nadu**; Tamil Nadu's winter rain comes from it {2009, 2008}
- Winter rain in the **northern plains**: **western disturbances** {1993, 2021}
- Western-disturbance rain **decreases from west to east** {2019}
- Area with winter rain: the **north-west** {2012}
- Northern plains get winter rain, but **not because of the NE monsoon** (R not the explanation) {?}

@trap
- **North-west** winter rain = **western disturbance**. **South-east** (Tamil Nadu) winter rain = **north-east (retreating) monsoon**. Never mix them.
@end

@section 9.5 | Local Storms and Seasons
- Match: **Mango shower-Kerala, Blossom shower-coastal Karnataka, Kal Baisakhi-West Bengal, Loo-Punjab to Bihar** {2024}
- Local storm of **Bengal and Assam**, good for tea and rice: **Norwesters (Kal Baisakhi)** {2023}
- Hindu seasons in order: **Vasanta (spring), Grishma (summer), Varsha (rain), Sharad (autumn)** {2021}

@table
Local wind | Region | Effect
Loo | Punjab to Bihar | Hot, dry, May-June
Kal Baisakhi | West Bengal, Assam | Thunder squalls; tea, jute, rice
Mango shower | Kerala, coastal Karnataka | Ripens mangoes
Blossom shower | Kerala, Karnataka | Coffee flowers
Western disturbance | North-west | Winter rain and snow
@end

@flow Six Hindu seasons (ritu)
Vasanta | Spring: March-May
Grishma | Summer: May-July
Varsha | Rains: July-September
Sharad | Autumn: September-November
Hemanta | Pre-winter: November-January
Shishira | Winter: January-March
@end

@up UP's rainfall falls from **east to west**: eastern UP gets about 100-150 cm, western UP about 60-80 cm, because the Bay of Bengal branch weakens as it moves up the Ganga plain. The **Loo** blows across UP in May-June; western UP gets **western-disturbance** rain in winter.

@last
- **Himalaya** makes India a monsoon land
- Monsoon reaches **Kerala** first (Andamans earliest)
- Monsoon = **south-west** winds
- **Tamil Nadu** dry in the SW monsoon; wet from the **NE monsoon**
- North-west winter rain = **western disturbance**, decreasing west to east
- **Leh** = least rain; **Mawsynram** = most
- **Loo** = Punjab to Bihar; **Kal Baisakhi** = Bengal-Assam
- **Mango shower** = Kerala; **blossom shower** = coffee
- Temperature falls with **altitude** (Agra vs Darjeeling)
- Seasons: Vasanta, Grishma, Varsha, Sharad, Hemanta, Shishira
@end
