@section 6.1 | Latent Heat and Temperature Scales
- Steam burns more than boiling water: steam carries **latent heat** {1993}
- **F = (9/5)C + 32** {2024}
- Celsius and Fahrenheit read the same at **-40** {2003, 2015}
- 98.4 F = **36.89 C** {2015}
- 200 F = **93.3 C** {2019}
- Averages follow the same rule: **F_{av} = (9/5)C_{av} + 32** {2016}
- Alcohol thermometers in cold countries: **lower freezing point** {1997}
- Antifreeze in car engines: **ethylene glycol** {2008, 2010}

@section 6.2 | Expansion and Cooling Devices
- Heating expands a body and **lowers its density** {2006}
- Glass rod in steam: width also **increases** {1994}
- Ice melting: volume **decreases** {2012}
- **Thermostat**: keeps **temperature** steady automatically {1993, 1996, ?}
- Safest fridge temperature for food: **4 C** {2007}
- Soggy biscuits turn crisp in a fridge: **low humidity** inside {2009}
- Ice cools a drink by taking **latent heat** (A and R true, R explains A) {2009}
- Ice in the freezer cuts cooling: ice is a **poor conductor** (A and R true, R explains A) {2010}
- Not true: AC and cooler **both keep humidity** (a cooler adds it, an AC removes it) {2007}

@section 6.3 | Humidity and Evaporation
- Relative humidity falls as temperature rises; evaporation raises absolute humidity (A and R true, R does not explain A) {2024}
- Clothes dry fastest at **20% RH and 60 C** {2008}
- Earthen pitcher keeps water cold by **evaporation** {2012}
- Sweating depends on **temperature and humidity** both {2019}
- Sweat evaporation removes heat; humidity adds discomfort (A and R true, R does not explain A) {2025}
- Humid heat felt in **humid weather** {2003}
- **Xeric** condition: **low humidity** {2014}

@section 6.4 | Pressure and Boiling Point
- We do not feel air pressure: **blood pressure** slightly exceeds it {2010}
- Water boils below 100 C at height: **air pressure falls** {1994}
- Boiling point falls with height; pressure **decreases** with height (A true, R false) {2023}
- Pressure cooker cooks fast: water boils at a **higher temperature** {2007, 2015}
- Cooker's top temperature depends on the **vent area and weight** {1995}
- Salt in water: boiling point **rises**, freezing point **falls** {2006}
- Bottle bursts on freezing: water **expands** as it freezes {1995}

@flow Pressure and boiling point
Hill station | Low pressure: water boils below 100 C; food cooks slowly
Sea level | 1 atm: water boils at 100 C
Pressure cooker | About 2 atm: boils near 120 C; cooks fast
Salt added | Boiling point up, freezing point down
@end

@section 6.5 | Conduction, Convection and Radiation
- Thermos flask: no heat loss by **conduction and convection** (both; silvered walls stop radiation) {2019}
- Best umbrella: **white on top, black below** {1993}
- White clothes in summer: they **reflect** heat {2007}
- Light clothes in summer: light colours absorb little heat (A and R true, R explains A) {2016}
- Cloudy nights are warmer: clouds **stop heat radiating** away {2008}
- Reflects most sunlight: **fresh snow** (highest albedo) {2021}
- Cold storage lengthens fruit life: **lower respiration** {2021}
- Wool keeps us warm, but it works by **trapping air**; the protein reason is false (A true, R false) {2001}

@trap
- Steam burns more because of **latent heat**, not higher temperature alone.
- On hills water boils **below** 100 C; in a cooker **above** 100 C.
- Salt raises boiling point but **lowers** freezing point.
- Ice melting **shrinks** in volume; water freezing **expands**.
@end
