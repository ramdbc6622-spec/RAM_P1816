@section 14.1 | Particles of the Atom
- Nucleus holds **protons and neutrons** {1996}
- Order of discovery: **electron, proton, neutron, pi meson** {2023}
- Neutron discovered by **James Chadwick** (1932) {1995, 1996}
- Not a sub-atomic particle: **deuteron** (a deuterium nucleus) {2007}
- Particle-antiparticle pair: **electron-positron** {2005}
- Zero charge: **neutrino** {2021}

@table

Proton | Rutherford (1919) | Positive
Neutron | Chadwick (1932) | None
Positron | Anderson (1932) | Positive (antielectron)
Neutrino | Predicted by Pauli (1930) | None
@end

@section 14.4 | Mixtures, Colloids and Solutions
- Salt solution is homogeneous; a homogeneous mix has the **same** composition throughout (A true, R false) {2022}
- Salt-ice mix goes below 0 C; salt **lowers**, not raises, the freezing point (A true, R false) {1998}
- Milk: an **emulsion** {2024, ?}
- Not a colloid: **honey** (a true solution) {2002}
- Dissolving 100 g sugar in half a litre of water: **volume** stays the same (per key) {2017}
- Osmosis: solvent flows from **pure solvent to solution**; reversing it is reverse osmosis (only 1) {2023}
- Not used to purify solid impurities: **distillation** (it is for liquids) {2016}
- Camphor, naphthalene, dry ice vanish: **sublimation** {2019}

@flow Kinds of mixtures
True solution | Particles under 1 nm; clear; salt or sugar in water, honey
Colloid | 1-1000 nm; milk, blood, fog, smoke, jelly
Emulsion | Liquid in liquid colloid; milk, butter, cream
Suspension | Particles settle; muddy water, chalk in water
@end

@trap
- Deuteron is a **nucleus**, not a fundamental sub-atomic particle.
- Salt **lowers** freezing point (salt-ice freezing mixtures).
- Honey is a true solution; milk is an emulsion.
- Sublimation = solid straight to vapour (camphor, naphthalene, dry ice, iodine).
@end
