@chapter 19 | Exchange Rate, FDI and External Debt
@meta Built from 41 UPPSC questions (1991–2024). Focus: devaluation, rupee convertibility, FDI sources and limits, external debt, forex reserves, the euro and FEMA.

@hot
- **Devaluation**: cut in a currency's value; **exports cheaper, imports costlier**; India: **1949** (first), 1966, **1991** (twice) {1991, 1994, 1998, 2001, 2003, 2004, 2008, 2008, 2012}
- Rupee **convertible on current account** (**1994**); capital account studied by **Tarapore** {2004, 2007, 2017}
- **FDI source**: **Mauritius** earlier, **Singapore** now (tax treaty change) {2006, 2010, 2024}
- **Forex reserves**: **foreign currency assets** are the largest part; **SDRs are included** {2001, 2002, 2023}
- **FERA** replaced by **FEMA**; fully in force **2002** {2007, 2013}
@end

@section 19.1 | Exchange Rate and Convertibility
- Flexible rate is set by demand and supply; that supply is **not** set by the central bank (A true, R false) {2024}
- Rupee convertibility now: for **current account** transactions only {2004}
- Fully convertible on current account: **1994** {2017}
- Full capital account convertibility: **Tarapore** Committee {2007}
- **Devaluation**: **reduction in value** against major currencies {1991, 1994, 1998}
- First devaluation: **1949** {2008}
- Devalued twice in **1991-92** {2012}
- Devaluation may promote exports as prices abroad fall (A and R true, R explains A) {2001, 2004}
- Effect: **imports costlier, exports cheaper** {2008}
- Aim: to fix the **balance of trade** {2003}
- Remittances 2022: **India 1st, Mexico 2nd, China 3rd, Philippines 4th** {2024}

@flow Rupee convertibility steps
1991 devaluation | Twice, 1 and 3 July 1991
LERMS 1992 | Partial convertibility, dual rate
March 1993 | Market-determined (managed float)
August 1994 | Current account convertibility (IMF Article VIII)
Tarapore 1997, 2006 | Capital account roadmap; not yet full
@end

@section 19.2 | Foreign Investment
- Most FDI in the last decade: **services** sector (telecom was taken as the answer in 2006) {2006}
- Large foreign investment came in **life insurance, banking, automobiles** (1, 2 and 3) {2009}
- Forex earners: **tourism, medical care, garments, leather** (all four) {2008}
- FPIs issue **participatory notes** to unregistered foreign investors {2019}
- FPI limit in corporate bonds (Budget 2020-21): **15%** {2020}
- Largest equity source: **Mauritius** when asked; **Singapore** now {2006, 2010}
- Singapore top FDI source in 2023-24 because of the Mauritius treaty change (A and R true, R explains A) {2024}
- FDI recently opened in **retail trade** {2010}
- Insurance FDI (2012 proposal): **26% to 49%** {2012}
- Foreign equity in small-scale units (1995-96): **24%** {1995}

@section 19.3 | External Debt
- External debt end-2010: over **US$ 300 billion** {2010}
- Largest component of external debt: **commercial borrowings** {2017}
- **ECBs** are regulated by the **RBI** {2010}
- Most indebted (World Bank 2010): **Russia, China, Turkey, Brazil, India** {2011}
- World Bank class for India: **less indebted** {2009}
- Not true of green bonds: that they are **short-maturity** loans {2016}
- **Oil Pool Account** closed: **1 April 2002** {2010}

@section 19.4 | Forex Reserves, the Euro and FEMA
- Not true: **SDRs are not included** (they are included) {2001}
- Largest share of reserves: **foreign currency assets** {2002}
- Reserves end-December 2024: **US$ 640.3 billion** {2023}
- Rupee value hit by **inflation, fiscal deficit, crude prices** (all three) {2013}
- New single European currency: **Euro** {1995}
- Not true of the euro in 1999: **in circulation** (notes came in 2002) {1999}
- Euro is the currency of **20** EU states (per key; 20 since Croatia joined, 2023) {2001}
- FERA replaced by **FEMA** {2007}
- FEMA finally implemented: **2002** (passed 1999, in force June 2000) {2013}
- Sanctions forex for imports: **exchange bank** (authorised dealer) {2011}
