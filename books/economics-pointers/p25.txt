@section 25.1 | Village, Town and Urban Growth
- City differs from village in **values, household make-up, way of living, economic activity** (all) {2004}
- Urban growth shows **all of the above** (more urban people, more towns, more income) {2004}
- Not an element of a rural community: **cultural diversity** {2021}
- **T.K. Oommen** on urban families: **all of the above** {2014}
- Causes of urbanisation: **migration, educational institutions, industrialisation** (1, 2, 3) {2015}
- Not in inclusive development (per key): **semi-urban** people {2016}
- Urban population by 2030 (key): **575 million** {2017}
- India: a **moderately low** urbanised country {1998}
- India is **not** over-urbanised; cities do lack infrastructure (A false, R true) {2017}
- India less urbanised than China; poor planning is not the reason (A and R true, R does not explain A) {2010}
- 2001 statements: urban 285 million, 27.78%, more than USA's population, self-reliant (all) {2002, 2003}
- India's urban people outnumber the USA's; US urbanisation is higher (A and R true, R does not explain A) {2000}
- India's urban population exceeds the USA's total (A and R true, R explains A, per key) {2002}
- Urban India is a platform for **all of the above** {2015}
- Fast urbanisation after 2001 and mobile revolution are unrelated (A and R true, R does not explain A) {2016}
- Not true: urbanisation has been **uniform** across regions {2002}
- Urbanisation and literacy both cut the **birth rate** (both) {2021}
- Urbanisation **reduced both birth and death rates** {2007}

@section 25.2 | Phases and Definitions
- Moderate urbanisation phase: **1931-61** {2003}
- Largest decadal urban growth: **1971-81** {2001, 2002, 2003, 2007, 2009}
- Not a criterion for a town: **physical expansion** {2008}
- Census-town tests: **5,000 people, 75% male non-farm workers, 400 per sq km** (1, 2, 3); no area test {2009}
- Census towns (2011): **3,894** {2010}
- Urban share 2011: **31.16%**, not in the options (none of the above) {2002, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2014, 2017, 2023}
- Most urban religion: **Jains** {2022}
- Fastest urban growth 2001-11 among options: **Sikkim** {2023}
- City dwellers (2011): about **37 crore** {2014}

@flow What makes a town (Census)
Statutory town | Municipality, corporation, cantonment or notified area
Census town test 1 | Population 5,000 or more
Census town test 2 | 75% or more of male workers outside farming
Census town test 3 | Density 400 or more per sq km
Census 2011 | 4,041 statutory + 3,894 census towns
@end

@section 25.4 | Rural India
- Fewest villages (UT): **Daman and Diu** {2014}
- Correct: urban increase exceeded rural, and 2001 urbanisation **27.81%** (1 and 3); 2011 rural share is **68.84%** {2010}
- Rural share (2001): **72.2%** {2008}
- Highest rural share: **Himachal Pradesh** {2015}
- Largest rural population: **Uttar Pradesh** {2018}
- Lowest rural child share: **Kerala** {2014}
- Lowest child sex ratio in both rural and urban areas: **Haryana** {2018}
- Most rural pucca houses (2001): **Punjab** {2008}
- Traders and makers mark the village-town line; they do not wholly depend on villages (A true, R false) {2009}
- **Occupational structure**: distribution of workers among **occupations** {2015}

@trap
- **Goa** = most urbanised state; **Tamil Nadu** = most urbanised **large** state; old keys often say Tamil Nadu.
- **Maharashtra** = most urban **people**; **Delhi** = most urbanised UT.
- Census town: no **area** test; 75% of **male** workers.
- 2001-11 = first decade when urban numbers grew more than rural.
@end

@up UP link: UP had the **second-largest urban population** (4.45 crore, 2011) but only **22.3%** urbanisation; it has the **largest rural population** (15.5 crore); UP has **17 municipal corporations** and many census towns around Noida-Ghaziabad.
