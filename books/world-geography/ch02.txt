@chapter 2 | The Earth and the Moon

@intro
This chapter covers the Earth as a planet (its tilt, composition, light-time from the Sun, and the parallelism of its axis) and its only natural satellite, the **Moon**. The Moon questions test simple physics: why we always see the same face, why sound cannot travel there, its phases, its distance, and why things weigh less on it. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2016
The Earth's axis of rotation (polar axis) is always inclined at an angle of
(a) 23.0o from the elliptic axis
(b) 23.1o from the elliptic axis
(c) 24.5o from the elliptic axis
(d) 23.5o from the elliptic axis
@ans (d)
@end

The axis is tilted about **23.5°** from the perpendicular to the plane of the orbit (the ecliptic). Put the other way, it makes **66.5°** with the orbital plane. The tilt, together with the **parallelism** of the axis, causes the seasons.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2000
Consider the following statements and select the correct answer from the codes given below:
1. The northern end of the earth's axis is called North Pole.
2. 45o latitude is half of the length of the equator.
3. There is parallelism of the earth's axis.
4. The earth's speed of revolution is faster in aphelion position.
Code :
(a) 1 and 2
(b) 2 and 3
(c) 3 and 4
(d) 1 and 3
@ans (d)
@end

Statement 2 is wrong because a parallel's length is the equator's length × cos(latitude). At 45° that is about **0.71**, not 0.5. The **60°** parallel is half the equator's length. Statement 4 is wrong because the Earth moves **fastest at perihelion** (January) and slowest at aphelion (July), as Kepler's second law says.

@section 2.2 | The Moon

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2006
Consider the following statements and answer the question with the help of codes given below :
Assertion (A) : Only one side of the moon's surface is visible from the earth.
Reason(R) : The period of rotation of the moon on its axis is equal to the period of its revolution around the earth.
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
@ans (a)
@end

The Moon is **tidally locked**. It turns once on its axis in the time it takes to go round the Earth, about **27.3 days**, so the same face always points towards us. Small wobbles (libration) let us see about **59%** of its surface over time.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2004
Unlike the fluid core of the Earth, the core of the moon is–
(a) Plasma
(b) Volatile gas
(c) Viscous liquid
(d) Solid
@ans (c)
@note The key follows the source. Seismic and orbital data now suggest the Moon has a small **solid inner core** inside a **fluid outer core**, broadly like the Earth's, but far smaller.
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2006
When any object is taken from the Earth to the Moon –
(a) Its weight increases
(b) Its weight decrease
(c) There is no change in its weight
(d) It becomes totally weightless
@ans (b)
@end

**Mass** stays the same everywhere, but **weight** on the Moon is about **one-sixth** of the weight on Earth, because the Moon's surface gravity is about 1.62 m/s².

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2008
The distance of Moon from the Earth is –
(a) 364 thousand km
(b) 300 thousand km
(c) 446 thousand km
(d) 350 thousand km
@ans (a)
@note The **average** Earth-Moon distance is about **3,84,400 km**. The orbit is elliptical, from about 3,63,000 km (perigee) to 4,05,000 km (apogee), and (a) is the closest of the options. A full moon near perigee is called a "**supermoon**".
@end
