@chapter 13 | The Atmosphere

@intro
Atmosphere questions are textbook basics: the **composition** of air, the **layers** in order (troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere with the ionosphere, exosphere) and what happens in each (weather, ozone, meteors, radio reflection, aurora). Then come **heat** (insolation, albedo), **pressure belts** (horse latitudes), **jet streams**, the **Coriolis force** and **clouds**. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

(a) Carbon dioxide
(b) Ozone
(c) Nitrogen
(d) Neon
@ans (c)

@table
Gas | Share of dry air (by volume)
Nitrogen | About 78.08%
Oxygen | About 20.95%
Argon | About 0.93%
Carbon dioxide | About 0.042% (about 420+ ppm, rising)
Neon, helium, methane, krypton, hydrogen, ozone | Traces

**Permanent gases** (nitrogen, oxygen, argon) keep a nearly constant share. **Variable gases** (water vapour, CO2, ozone) change from place to place and over time.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2005
The four layers of the atmosphere are –
1. Ionosphere
2. Mesosphere
3. Stratosphere
4. Troposphere
Their correct ascending order in terms of height is
Code :
(a) 1, 2, 3, 4
(b) 2, 1, 4, 3
(c) 4, 3, 2, 1
(d) 3, 4, 1, 2
@ans (a)
@note The Commission's key is (a), but that is the **descending** order (top to bottom). Going upward from the ground (ascending in height) the order is **Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Ionosphere = 4, 3, 2, 1**, which is option **(c)**. Use (c) if the question is asked again with the word "ascending".
@end

Which of the following are correct in matching the layer of atmosphere and its characteristics ?

(a) 1, 2, 3 and 4

(c) 1, 2, and 3

@table
Layer | Height (approx.) | Temperature trend | Key feature
Troposphere | 0 to about 8 km (poles) / 18 km (equator) | Falls about 6.5 °C per km | All **weather**, clouds, most water vapour

Exosphere | Above about 500 km | - | Thin outer fringe; gases escape to space
@end

@section 13.2 | Heat and Pressure

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1997
Assertion (A) : The atmosphere receives most of the heat only indirectly from the sun and directly from the earth's surface.
Reason (R) : The conversion from shortwave solar to long wave terrestrial energy takes place at the earth's surface.
Code:
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
(d) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
@ans (a)
@end

The air is heated **from below**. Short-wave sunlight passes through, warms the ground, and the ground re-radiates **long-wave** (infrared) heat that greenhouse gases absorb. That is why the troposphere is warmest near the surface.

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 2019
Which one of the following is associated with 'Albedo'?
(a) Transmitting power
(b) Absorbing power
(c) Emissive power
(d) Reflecting power
@ans (d)
@end

**Albedo** is the fraction of sunlight a surface reflects: fresh snow about 0.8-0.9, deserts about 0.3-0.4, forests and oceans about 0.06-0.15. The Earth's average is about **0.30**. Melting ice lowers albedo and speeds up warming (the ice-albedo feedback).

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2002
Which of the following statements is not true:
(a) Presence of water vapour is highly variable in the lower atmosphere.
(b) The zone of maximum temperature is located along the equator
(c) Frigid zones are located in both the hemispheres between the polar circles and the poles.
(d) Jet streams are high altitude winds affecting the surface weather conditions.
@ans (b)
@end

The hottest belt, the **thermal equator**, lies north of the geographical Equator, at about **10-20°N** on average, because the Northern Hemisphere has more land. The highest temperatures are recorded in the **subtropical deserts**, not at the cloudy Equator.

@table
Pressure belt | Latitude | Winds / note
Equatorial low (Doldrums, ITCZ) | 0-5° | Calm, rising air, heavy rain
Subtropical high (**Horse latitudes**) | About **30-35°** N and S | Calm, sinking dry air; the hot deserts
Subpolar low | About 60-65° | Meeting of westerlies and polar easterlies; storms

@end

The name "horse latitudes" is said to come from sailing ships becalmed there, which threw horses overboard to save water.

@section 13.3 | Winds, Jet Streams and Clouds

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2023
What caused wind to deflect towards left in Southern Hemisphere?
(a) Magnetic Field
(b) Coriolis force
(c) Temperature
(d) Pressure
@ans (b)
@end

The **Coriolis force**, caused by the Earth's rotation, turns winds to the **right in the Northern** Hemisphere and to the **left in the Southern** (**Ferrel's law**). It is zero at the Equator and strongest at the poles, which is why cyclones do not form within about 5° of the Equator.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2024
Given below are two statements, one is labelled as Assertion (A) and the other as Reason (R).
Assertion (A): Jet streams discovered during World War II are high altitude easterly winds.
Reason (R): Jet streams flow with a speed of 300-500 km/hour.
Select the correct answer from the codes given below:
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
(c) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(d) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
@ans (b)
@end

**Jet streams** are fast, narrow bands of **westerly** wind near the tropopause (about 9-15 km). They were noticed by WWII bomber pilots over Japan. The **subtropical westerly jet** sits south of the Himalaya in winter and moves north in summer, which helps the **monsoon** to burst. The **tropical easterly jet** over India in summer is the main exception to the westerly rule.

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Pre) 2009

(a) Altocumulus
(b) Altostratus
(c) Cumulus
(d) Cirrostratus
@ans (d)
@end

Cloud family | Height (approx.) | Types
High clouds | Above 6 km | **Cirrus**, **cirrostratus** (halo), cirrocumulus ("mackerel sky")

@pattern
These are mostly **one-line definition** questions: which gas, which layer, which feature (ozone in the stratosphere, radio and aurora in the ionosphere), albedo, horse latitudes and the Coriolis force. The 2023-2024 papers added **jet streams** and **Coriolis**, so expect more process-based questions. One old key (layer order) is reversed; see its note.
@end
