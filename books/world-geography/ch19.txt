@chapter 19 | Industrial Cities and Regions of the World

@intro
World-industry questions are **city-to-industry** and **region-to-country** matches. The same names recur: Pittsburgh, Detroit, Hamilton, Birmingham, Sheffield, Essen, Anshan, Magnitogorsk, Osaka, Nagoya, Yokohama, Glasgow and Dundee. The other topic is **location theory**: raw-material, market and footloose industries, and why Japan's steel mills sit on the coast. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@pyq U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Spl) (Pre) 2010; U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2013
Which country has market-based locations of iron and steel industries ?
(a) China
(b) India
(c) Japan
(d) U.K.
@ans (c)
@end

**Japan** has almost no iron ore or coking coal, so it imports both. Its steel mills stand at **coastal ports** near the big markets (Tokyo-Yokohama, Osaka-Kobe, Nagoya, Kitakyushu). This is the classic **market- and port-based** location. India's older plants (Jamshedpur, Bhilai, Rourkela) are **raw-material based**, built near the iron ore and coal.

@section 19.4 | Textiles and Industrial Location

@pyq U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2013
Which one is the biggest wool-producing country in the world?
(a) China
(b) U.S.A.
(c) Australia
(d) U.K.
@ans (a)
@end

**China** leads in total greasy wool, followed by **Australia** and New Zealand. Australia leads in **fine Merino wool** and in **wool exports**.

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Mains) 2008

@table
Location type | Pulled towards | Examples
Raw-material oriented (weight-losing) | Source of bulky raw material | Iron and steel (old plants), sugar, cement, pulp
Market oriented (weight-gaining or perishable) | Big cities and ports | Bread, soft drinks, Japanese steel, refineries near cities
Power oriented | Cheap electricity | Aluminium smelters (Renukoot, Norway, Canada)
**Footloose** | Skilled labour, good communications | **Software, electronics**, diamond cutting, watches
@end

@trap
**Birmingham is steel**, not shipbuilding; **Glasgow** and **Belfast** build ships. **Detroit is automobiles**, not cotton. **Houston is oil and space**, not automobiles. **Moscow and Chelyabinsk** are inland and build no ships, while **St. Petersburg** does. **Camden** is in **New Jersey**, not Florida. **Mesabi is iron ore**, not coal.
@end

@heatmap

@pattern
Nearly every question here is a **city-industry** match, and the US, UK, Japanese and Soviet centres listed above account for almost all of them. The papers from 2015 onwards add **industrial-region** matching (Kinki, Canton, Belo Horizonte, Flanders) and **location theory** (footloose, market-based).
@end
