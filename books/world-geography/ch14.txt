@chapter 14 | Cyclones, Tornadoes and Local Winds

@intro
This chapter has two halves. **Storms**: the regional names of tropical cyclones (hurricane, typhoon, willy-willy, baguio), tornadoes and "Tornado Alley", the scales (Fujita, Saffir-Simpson), and cyclones in the news. **Local winds**: the hot and cold winds of the Alps, the Mediterranean, the deserts and the Rockies (Foehn, Chinook, Sirocco, Mistral, Bora, Khamsin, Harmattan, Brickfielder, Shamal). Both halves are almost always tested as **match-the-pair** questions. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@table
Region | Name of the tropical cyclone
North Atlantic, Caribbean, NE Pacific (USA, Mexico) | **Hurricane**
NW Pacific (China, Japan, Taiwan) | **Typhoon** (Taifu in Japan)
Philippines | **Baguio**
NW Australia | **Willy-willy**
North Indian Ocean (India, Bangladesh), South Indian Ocean | **Cyclone**
@end

A tropical cyclone needs sea water warmer than about **26.5-27 °C**, enough **Coriolis force** (so not within about 5° of the Equator), low wind shear and an existing disturbance. It turns **anticlockwise** in the Northern Hemisphere and **clockwise** in the Southern. Its calm centre is the **eye**.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2005
Which one of the following regions of the U.S.A. is known as "Tornado Alley"?
(a) Atlantic Seaboard
(b) Pacific Coast
(c) Mississippi Plains
(d) Alaska
@ans (c)
@end

A **tornado** is a small (tens to hundreds of metres wide), violent spinning column of air hanging from a thunderstorm. Strictly, it is not a tropical cyclone. The **USA** has the most tornadoes in the world, in "Tornado Alley" across **Texas, Oklahoma, Kansas and Nebraska**, where cold, dry Canadian air meets warm, moist air from the Gulf of Mexico. Over water a tornado becomes a **waterspout**.

(b) Mercalli Scale
(c) Fujita Scale

@ans (a)
@end

@table
Scale | Measures
Fujita / Enhanced Fujita (EF0-EF5) | **Tornado** intensity, judged from damage
Saffir-Simpson (Category 1-5) | **Hurricane** wind speed and expected damage
IMD scale (Depression to Super Cyclonic Storm) | Cyclones in the North Indian Ocean
Richter / moment magnitude | Earthquake **magnitude**
Modified Mercalli (I-XII) | Earthquake **intensity**
Beaufort (0-12) | Wind force at sea and on land
@end

@pyq U.P. U.D.A./L.D.A. (Pre) 2013
Which of the following cyclones did not affect India?
(a) Aila
(b) Nargis
(c) Thane
(d) Neelam
@ans (b)
@end

**Nargis** (2008) devastated **Myanmar**'s Irrawaddy delta. **Aila** (2009) hit West Bengal and Bangladesh, **Thane** (2011) and **Neelam** (2012) hit Tamil Nadu. Cyclone names in the North Indian Ocean come from a list contributed by **13 countries** (WMO/ESCAP Panel). Recent storms include Amphan (2020), Biparjoy (2023), Michaung (2023), Remal (2024), Dana (2024) and Fengal (2024).

@section 14.2 | Planetary and Local Winds

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1992
The winds flowing from high-pressure area toward the Mediterranean sea are
(a) Westerly
(b) Trade wind
(c) Monsoonal wind
(d) Sea wind
@ans (*)
@note The Mediterranean lies at the shifting edge of the subtropical high. **Westerlies** bring its **winter** rain and **trade winds** (offshore) bring its dry **summer**, so both (a) and (b) fit depending on the season. The source marks the question (*). The commonly expected answer is (a) westerlies, which explain the winter rain.
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2016
Which one of the following pairs is not correctly matched?
(a) Foehn - Alps Mountain
(b) Bora - Poland
(c) Mistral - Rhine Valley
(d) Khamsin - Egypt
@ans (b)
@note The **Bora** blows down to the **Adriatic** coast (Croatia, Slovenia, NE Italy), not Poland, which makes (b) the key. Note that the **Mistral** actually funnels down the **Rhone** valley of southern France. "Rhine" in option (c) is a slip, so (c) is also strictly wrong.
@end

@table
Local wind | Type | Region
Foehn | Warm, dry, descending | North slopes of the **Alps** (Switzerland, Austria); melts snow
Chinook ("snow eater") | Warm, dry, descending | East of the **Rockies** (USA, Canada)
Santa Ana | Hot, dry | **Southern California**; fans wildfires
Sirocco | Hot, dusty, from the Sahara | **Libya to Italy, Sicily, Malta**; picks up moisture and gives red "**blood rain**"
Khamsin | Hot, dry, dusty | **Egypt**
Leveche | Hot, dry | **Spain**
Harmattan ("the doctor") | Dry, dusty north-easterly | West Africa (Guinea coast)
Haboob | Violent dust storm | **Sudan** (and Arizona)
Simoom | Hot, suffocating desert wind | Arabia, Sahara
Shamal | Hot, dry north-westerly | **Iraq, Iran, Arabian Peninsula** (Persian Gulf)
Brickfielder | Hot, dusty | **South-east Australia** (Victoria)
Mistral | Cold, northerly | **Rhone valley**, France
Bora | Cold, dry, gusty | **Adriatic** coast (Croatia, Italy)
Blizzard | Cold, snowy storm | **Canada, USA**, Siberia (buran)
Black roller | Dusty squall | Great Plains of **North America**
Yamo | Hot, dry | **Japan**
Purga / Buran | Snow blizzard | Siberia (Russia)
Norwesters (Kalbaisakhi) | Thunderstorm squalls | **West Bengal, Assam, Odisha**, Bangladesh (pre-monsoon)
Loo | Hot, dry | **North India** plains (May-June)
@end

@trap
**Mistral = France (Rhone)**, not Australia or the Rhine. **Shamal = Iraq and the Gulf**, not Austria. **Bora = Adriatic**, not Poland. **Blizzard = Canada and USA**, not Chile. **Sirocco** blows **from** the Sahara **to** Italy and Sicily, so "Sirocco-France" is the wrong pair. **Chinook and Foehn** are both warm, descending **"snow eaters"**, one in North America and the other in the Alps.
@end
