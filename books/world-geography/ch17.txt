@chapter 17 | World Agriculture and Livestock

@intro
This is the longest chapter in the World Geography book. Most questions ask **"who is the largest producer"** of a crop: rice, wheat, sugarcane, beet, cotton, coconut, rubber, coffee, tea, cocoa, maize or milk. These rankings change with each FAO release, so several old keys now carry notes with **FAO 2024** positions. Other topics are **crop-region** matches (Sao Paulo coffee, Ghana cocoa, Yangtze rice), **farming systems** (plantation, shifting cultivation and its local names, alley cropping, monoculture) and livestock. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

Under the international agreement on agriculture the Green Box includes economic assistance for
1. Agricultural Research

3. Irrigation
4. Plant Protection

(a) 1 and 2
(b) 2 and 3
(c) 3 and 4
(d) 1 and 4
@ans (d)
@end

Under the WTO **Agreement on Agriculture**:
- **Green Box** subsidies do not distort trade or do so minimally: research, pest control, extension, environment, disaster relief.
- **Amber Box** subsidies distort trade (input subsidies, price support) and are capped at **10%** of output value for developing countries.
- **Blue Box** payments are tied to production limits.

@trap
**Two-crop leader traps**: "both rice and wheat" no longer has one answer (India leads rice, China leads wheat). **Tea exports**: China leads by value, Kenya by volume. **Sugar**: Brazil leads in cane, Russia/France in beet, and Ukraine makes sugar almost only from beet. **Chena = Sri Lanka**, **Ladang = Indonesia/Malaysia**, and **Fazenda** is a coffee estate, not shifting cultivation.
@end

@heatmap

@pattern
About **half** of this chapter's PYQs ask "largest producer / exporter", so the answers **change with FAO data**. Always check the notes for the 2024 position. The rest are crop-region matches and farming-system names. The newer papers (2017-2023) favour **exports by country** (Chad, Niger, Rwanda, Benin) and **shifting-cultivation names**.
@end

@next
Likely next angles: India becoming the **top rice producer** and its export curbs and their removal (2022-24), **cocoa price spike** (Ghana, Cote d'Ivoire), **coffee** and the **EU deforestation rules**, **millets** (International Year 2023; India the largest producer), the **WTO** public-stockholding peace clause, and **Golden Crescent** drug routes.
@end
