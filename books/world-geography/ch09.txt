@chapter 9 | Oceans, Seas and Ocean Currents
@unit UNIT IV · HYDROSPHERE AND ATMOSPHERE

@intro
The **hydrosphere** chapter covers the world's water budget, the oceans and their marginal seas (Red, Black, Caspian, Aral, Tyrrhenian, Sargasso), **ocean currents** (warm vs cold, which ocean), **salinity**, **tides** and the **deep trenches**. Current questions follow a pattern: currents flowing **towards the poles** along **eastern** coasts are warm, and those flowing **towards the equator** along **western** coasts are cold. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@pyq U.P.U.D.A./L.D.A. (Spl) (Mains) 2010
The tension of gravitational water in the ground is –
(a) below 1/3 atmosphere
(b) At 1.25 atmosphere
(c) At 5 atmosphere
(d) At 15 atmosphere
@ans (a)
@end

**Gravitational water** drains freely through the soil under gravity (tension below 1/3 atm). **Capillary water** is held in the pores (1/3 to 31 atm) and is what plants use. **Hygroscopic water** is bound tightly to particles and is unavailable to plants.

The **Periplus of the Erythraean Sea** (1st century AD) is a Greek sailing guide to the Red Sea, Arabian Sea and Indian trade ports such as Barygaza (Bharuch) and Muziris. The Indian Ocean is the **only ocean named after a country**, and it is the third largest after the Pacific and Atlantic.

@pyq U.P. P.C.S. (Pre) 2017

(a) Volcanic valley
(b) Eroded valley
(c) Axial trough
(d) U-shaped valley
@ans (c)
@end

The **Red Sea** is a young ocean basin opening along the **Afro-Arabian rift**, part of the same system as the East African Rift. Hot, dry air and almost no river inflow make it one of the **saltiest** open seas (about 36-41‰).

The **Sargasso Sea** is the only sea **bounded by currents, not land**: the Gulf Stream, the North Atlantic Drift, the Canary Current and the North Equatorial Current enclose it. It is known for floating **Sargassum** seaweed and as the breeding ground of the European and American **eels**.

@trap
**Benguela is Atlantic** (off Namibia), and **Agulhas is Indian Ocean** (off south-east Africa). Both are near South Africa, which is the trap. **Humboldt is South Pacific**, not North Pacific. **Canary is North Atlantic**, so it is not part of the South Atlantic gyre. Where a warm and a cold current meet, there are **fog and rich fishing grounds**: the Grand Banks (Gulf Stream + Labrador) and Japan (Kuroshio + Oyashio).
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1995
The highest salinity is found in :
(a) Dead sea
(b) Red sea
(c) Great Salt Lake in the U.S.A.
(d) Lake Van in Turkey
@ans (d)
@note The key follows older Indian textbooks, which gave **Lake Van** about 330‰. Modern measurements put Lake Van, a soda lake, at only about **22‰**, and the **Dead Sea** at about **340‰**, making the Dead Sea the most saline of the four. Very salty smaller lakes include **Don Juan Pond** (Antarctica) and **Lake Assal** (Djibouti).
@end

@one U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) (Re-Exam) 2015
What denotes water salinity gradient?
@ans Halocline
@end

Open-ocean salinity averages about **35‰** and is highest near the **tropics** (about 20-30°), where evaporation is high and rainfall low. It is lower at the equator (heavy rain) and near the poles (melting ice). The layers of rapid change with depth are the **halocline** (salt), **thermocline** (temperature) and **pycnocline** (density).

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 1999
Spring Tide occurs :
(a) When the Sun the Earth and Moon are in a straight line
(b) When the Sun and Moon make a right angle
(c) When a strong wind blows
(d) When the night is very cold
@ans (a)
@end

**Spring tides** (highest range) come at **new and full moon**, when the Sun, Earth and Moon are in line. **Neap tides** (lowest range) come at the **quarter moons**, when the Sun and Moon are at **right angles**. Tides recur about every **12 h 25 min**, because the Moon rises about 50 minutes later each day. The world's highest tides are in the **Bay of Fundy** (Canada).
