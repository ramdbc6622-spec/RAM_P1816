@intro
UPPSC opens World Geography with the universe. The questions are short and factual: the **Big Bang**, galaxies and black holes, the order and records of the **planets** (largest, smallest, hottest, brightest, ringed, moonless), famous space missions, and the **Sun** as a fusion reactor. Many older keys still count **Pluto** as a planet, so keep the **2006 IAU** reclassification in mind. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.

@section 1.1 | The Universe, Galaxies and Stars

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2016
The ''Big-Bang Theory'' explains the origin of –
(a) Mammals
(b) Ice-Age
(c) Universe
(d) Ocean
@ans (c)
@end

The **Big Bang** idea was proposed by **Georges Lemaitre** (1927). **Edwin Hubble**'s discovery that galaxies are moving apart (1929) supported it, and **Fred Hoyle**, a critic, coined the name "Big Bang" (1949). The universe is about **13.8 billion years** old.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2001
The Milky Way is classified as –
(a) Spiral Galaxy
(b) Electrical Galaxy
(c) Irregular Galaxy
(d) Round Galaxy
@ans (a)
@end

The **Milky Way** is a **barred spiral** galaxy about 1 lakh light-years across. The Sun lies in the **Orion Arm**, about 26,000 light-years from the centre. Our nearest large neighbour is the **Andromeda** galaxy (M31).

@table
Stage or object | What it is
Nebula | Cloud of gas and dust where stars are born
Main-sequence star | Star fusing hydrogen into helium (the Sun now)
Red giant / supergiant | Swollen star near the end of its life (Betelgeuse)
White dwarf | Remnant core below the **Chandrasekhar limit** (about 1.4 solar masses)
Supernova | Explosion of a dying massive star
Neutron star / Pulsar | Super-dense remnant; a pulsar sends out regular radio pulses
Black hole | Remnant so dense that not even light escapes
Quasar | Extremely bright core of a distant galaxy powered by a black hole
@end

**S. Chandrasekhar** worked out the limit in **1930-31** and shared the **1983 Nobel Prize** in Physics for it. Stellar remnants heavier than the limit collapse into neutron stars or black holes. The first image of a black hole (M87*) was released by the **Event Horizon Telescope** in 2019.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2004
Hydrogen, Helium and Methane are the main gases present on
(a) Uranus, Neptune and Pluto
(b) Jupiter, Saturn and Mars
(c) Uranus, Neptune and Venus
(d) Mars and Venus
@ans (*)
@note Hydrogen, helium and methane are found together on the giant planets. Methane gives **Uranus and Neptune** their blue colour. **Pluto** has a thin nitrogen-methane atmosphere but no hydrogen-helium envelope, and **Mars** and **Venus** have mostly **carbon dioxide**. No option is fully correct, so the source marks it (*).
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2008
The Asteroids are small celestial bodies in the solar system which are found between the planets.
(a) Mercury and Venus
(b) Mars and Jupiter
(c) Jupiter and Saturn
(d) Neptune and Saturn
@ans (b)
@end

The **main asteroid belt** lies between **Mars and Jupiter**. Its largest body, **Ceres** (about 950 km), is now classed as a dwarf planet. The other dwarf planets are Pluto, Eris, Haumea and Makemake. Beyond Neptune lies the **Kuiper Belt**, and farther still the **Oort Cloud**, the source of long-period comets.

@trap
**Brightest = Venus**, **largest = Jupiter**, **largest moon = Ganymede**, not Titan. **Hottest = Venus**, not Mercury, because of its thick CO2 greenhouse. **No moons = Mercury and Venus.** **Day and tilt like Earth's = Mars.** For any "Pluto" option, check whether the question predates 2006.
@end
