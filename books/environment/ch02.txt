@table
Trophic level | Role | Examples
T1 Producers (autotrophs) | Make food by photosynthesis; link the abiotic and biotic worlds | Green plants, **phytoplankton** (in the sea), algae
T2 Primary consumers | Herbivores | Deer, cow, grasshopper, ants, zooplankton
T3 Secondary consumers | Carnivores that eat herbivores | Frog, fox, small fish
T4 Tertiary / top consumers | Top carnivores | Snake, hawk, tiger, shark
Omnivores | Eat at more than one level | **Humans**, bear, crow
Decomposers | Break down dead matter and return nutrients | Bacteria, fungi
@end

A **grazing food chain** starts from living plants (grass, grasshopper, frog, snake, hawk). A **detritus food chain** starts from dead organic matter (leaf litter, earthworm, bird) and carries most of the energy in forests. Linked food chains form a **food web**, which gives an ecosystem stability.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2025
With reference to 'Ecosystem', which of the following statements is/are correct?
1. Green plants in terrestrial ecosystem capture about 1% of energy of the sunlight that falls on their leaves.
2. Around 10% of organic matter reaches to the next higher level of consumers.
Select the correct answer from the code given below :
Code :
(a) Only 2
(b) Neither 1 nor 2
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Only 1
@ans (c)
@end

**Raymond Lindeman** (1942) showed that only about **10%** of the energy at one trophic level passes to the next. The rest is lost as heat in respiration, by the **second law of thermodynamics**. Plants fix only about **1-2%** of the sunlight that reaches them. That is why food chains rarely have more than **4-5 links**, and why energy flow is **one-way**, unlike the cycling of nutrients.

@section 2.4 | Ecological Pyramids and Productivity

@pyq U.P.P.S.C. (GIC) 2010

(a) Forest
(b) Pond
(c) Grassland
(d) Drylands

@end

@table
Pyramid | Usually | Inverted when
Pyramid of **numbers** | Upright (many grass plants, fewer deer, one tiger) | **Tree ecosystem** (one tree, many insects); parasite chains
Pyramid of **biomass** | Upright on land | **Pond / ocean**: the small, fast-reproducing phytoplankton weigh less than the zooplankton and fish feeding on them
Pyramid of **energy** | **Always upright** | Never, because energy is lost at every step
@end

@pyq U.P. R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) 2021
Which one of the following is the correct sequence of ecosystems in the order of decreasing productivity?
(a) Oceans, lakes, grasslands, mangroves
(b) Mangroves, oceans, grasslands, lakes
(c) Mangroves, grasslands, lakes, oceans
(d) Oceans, mangroves, lakes, grasslands

@end

Per unit area, **estuaries, mangroves, coral reefs and tropical rainforests** are the most productive ecosystems. The **open ocean** and **deserts** are the least productive. The ocean's total output is large only because it is so vast.

@up UP link: The **Chipko movement** began in **1973** at **Mandal** and **Reni** villages of Chamoli, then in **Uttar Pradesh** and now in Uttarakhand. It was led by **Gaura Devi**, **Chandi Prasad Bhatt** and **Sunderlal Bahuguna**, whose slogan was "Ecology is permanent economy". **Appiko** (1983) was its Karnataka counterpart.

@trap
**Energy flows one way, while nutrients cycle.** The **pyramid of energy is never inverted**, and the **pond's biomass pyramid is**. **Lotic = flowing** (rivers), **lentic = still** (ponds, lakes). Ecological footprint is measured in **global hectares**. The **10% law** is Lindeman's, not Odum's or Tansley's.
@end
