@chapter 8 | Forests and Their Types
@unit UNIT IV · FORESTS AND PROTECTED AREAS

@intro
This chapter covers **forest types** (tropical evergreen, deciduous or monsoon, coniferous, mangroves) and where they occur in India: the Western Ghats evergreen belt, the **Sundarbans**, **Bhitarkanika** and the Goa mangroves. It also covers biomass and productivity, and the **assertion-reason** questions on forests as a renewable resource, the hydrological cycle and the causes of forest loss. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 8.1 | Forest Types, Diversity and Productivity

@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Re. Exam) (Pre) 2015
Which of the following has the lowest mean net primary productivity?
(a) Temperate forests
(b) Tropical forests
(c) Temperate grasslands
(d) Tropical savanna
@ans (c)
@end

**Net primary productivity** (NPP) is the energy that plants store after subtracting what they use in respiration. Average NPP per year is roughly: tropical rainforest about 2,200 g/m², temperate forest about 1,250, savanna about 900, **temperate grassland about 600**, and desert under 100.

(b) Asia
(c) Australia

@ans (b)
@end

@table
Forest type (India) | Rainfall | Where | Typical trees
**Tropical evergreen** | Over 200 cm | **Western Ghats**, NE India, Andaman and Nicobar | Rosewood, ebony, mahogany, white cedar
Tropical semi-evergreen | 150-200 cm | Fringes of the evergreen belt | Mixed
**Tropical moist deciduous** (monsoon) | 100-200 cm | NE plateau, Shivalik foothills, Odisha, Jharkhand | **Sal, teak**, sandalwood, bamboo
Tropical dry deciduous | 70-100 cm | Central India, UP, Bihar | Teak, tendu, palas, khair
**Thorn forests** | Under 70 cm | **Rajasthan**, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana | **Acacia (babul)**, khejri, cactus
Montane (temperate / **coniferous**) | Varies with height | **Himalaya** (Himachal, J&K, Uttarakhand), Nilgiri **sholas** | **Deodar, pine, fir, spruce**, oak
**Mangroves (tidal)** | Coastal deltas | **Sundarbans**, Bhitarkanika, Godavari-Krishna, Gulf of Kutch, A&N | **Sundari**, Rhizophora, Avicennia
@end

India's forest types were classified by **Champion and Seth** (1968) into **16 type groups** and about 200 sub-types. **Tropical dry deciduous** forest covers the largest area, followed by **tropical moist deciduous**.

@pyq U.P.R.O./A.R.O. (Pre) 2016
Consider the following statements about Bhitarkanika Mangroves:
1. It is located in the deltaic region of Vansadhara and Subarnarekha rivers.
2. It lies in the state of West Bengal.
Of these:
(a) Only 1 is correct.
(b) Only 2 is correct.
(c) 1 and 2 both are correct
(d) Neither 1 nor 2 is correct.
@ans (d)
@end

**Bhitarkanika** (Kendrapara, **Odisha**) lies in the delta of the **Brahmani, Baitarani and Mahanadi**. It is India's second-largest mangrove forest, a Ramsar site, and home to the largest population of **saltwater crocodiles** in India. **Gahirmatha**, next to it, is the world's largest nesting beach for **olive ridley turtles**. **Goa**'s **Salim Ali Bird Sanctuary** on **Chorao Island** protects mangroves on the Mandovi.

@pyq U.P. Lower Sub. (Spl) (Pre) 2006; U.P.P.S.C. (GIC) 2010
Assertion (A) : Coastal Odisha is most cyclone-prone area of India.
Reason (R) : There is a large amount of deforestation of Mangroves in Mahanadi delta area.
Choose the correct answer in the given codes :
Code :
(a) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(b) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(c) (A) is false, but (R) is true.
(d) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
@ans (a)
@note The key links the two, since losing mangroves removes a natural cyclone barrier and makes the coast more **vulnerable**. Strictly, Odisha is cyclone-prone because of where it sits on the **Bay of Bengal** cyclone tracks; mangrove loss increases the **damage** rather than the number of cyclones. Some would argue for (b).
@end

India's mangrove cover is about **4,990 sq km** (ISFR 2023), roughly 0.15% of the country and about 3% of South Asia's mangroves. **West Bengal** (Sundarbans) has the most, followed by **Gujarat** and the **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**. The **MISHTI** scheme (2023) promotes mangrove planting.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2011
The main causes for the forest loss are –
(a) Road Development
(b) River Valley projects
(c) Industrial Development
(d) Agricultural Development
@ans (c)
@note The key is (c), following the source. Many global studies (FAO) name **agricultural expansion** as the leading cause of deforestation, which would be option (d). In India, mining, industry and infrastructure take most of the land diverted under the Forest Conservation Act.
@end

@trap
**Bhitarkanika is in Odisha** (Brahmani-Baitarani delta), not West Bengal. **Evergreen forests = Western Ghats**, not the Eastern Ghats or plateaus. **Coniferous = Himalaya**, **thorn/deciduous = Rajasthan**. **Temperate grassland** has the lowest NPP of the four options. The richest forest for biomass and plant diversity is the **tropical rainforest**.
@end
