@chapter 12 | Biosphere Reserves, Wildlife Sanctuaries and Project Tiger

@intro
This chapter completes India's protected-area network. It covers India's **18 biosphere reserves** (13 in UNESCO's network) and their states, the best-known **wildlife sanctuaries** (Periyar, Namdapha, the Wild Ass Sanctuary, National Chambal) and **Project Tiger** (1973): tiger reserves by state, the largest reserve, tiger censuses and Tiger Day. UP's own reserves (**Dudhwa, Pilibhit, Amangarh, Ranipur**) appear in UP-link notes. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

The word "**biosphere**" was coined by **Eduard Suess** (1875). A **biosphere reserve**, under UNESCO's **Man and the Biosphere (MAB)** programme (1971), has three zones: a strictly protected **core**, a **buffer** where research and limited use are allowed, and an outer **transition** zone where people live and farm.

@pyq U.P.P.C.S (Pre) 2011
The World Tiger Summit, 2010 was held at –
(a) Bangkok
(b) Nairobi
(c) New Delhi

@ans (d)
@end

**Project Tiger** began on **1 April 1973** at **Corbett** with **9 reserves**. It is now run by the **National Tiger Conservation Authority** (2006). The **St. Petersburg Tiger Summit (2010)** set the **TX2** goal of doubling wild tigers by 2022, which India met early, and declared **29 July** as **International Tiger Day**.

@up UP link: UP has **four tiger reserves**: **Dudhwa** (1987, with Kishanpur and Katarniaghat), **Pilibhit** (2014, which won the first global **TX2 award** in 2020 for doubling its tigers), **Amangarh** (Bijnor, the buffer of Corbett) and **Ranipur** (Chitrakoot, 2022, UP's 4th).
