@chapter 9 | India State of Forest Report (ISFR)

@intro
The **Forest Survey of India** (Dehradun) publishes the **India State of Forest Report** every two years from satellite data. Its figures change with every edition, so this chapter's old keys need careful notes. The newest report is **ISFR 2023** (released December 2024). Learn its headline numbers: total cover, state rankings by **area** and by **percentage**, very dense forest, mangroves and tree cover. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@section 9.3 | Uttar Pradesh

@pyq U.P.P.C.S. (Mains) 2012
The recorded forest area of U.P. in 2011 was around .... of its area :
(a) 3%
(b) 5%
(c) 7%
(d) 9%
@ans (c)
@end

@up UP link: **Uttar Pradesh** has **recorded forest area** of about 17,435 sq km (**7.24%** of the state), and its **forest cover** is only about **6%** of its area. Adding **tree cover** (about 3%) brings the total to roughly **9-10%**. UP was among the states with the largest **gain in tree cover** in ISFR 2023, helped by its mass plantation drives (more than **35 crore saplings** planted in a single campaign in 2024). The **Terai** districts (Lakhimpur Kheri, Bahraich, Pilibhit) and **Sonbhadra** are the most forested. **Dudhwa** is UP's best-known forest-protected area.

@trap
"**Forest area**" (MP is the largest) is different from "**forest percentage**" (Mizoram is the highest state). **Very dense forest = Arunachal.** **Least = Haryana.** "**Recorded** forest area" (legal status) is different from "**forest cover**" (canopy seen from satellites). For ISFR questions, the report year matters: the answer for 2015, 2017, 2019, 2021 and 2023 can differ.
@end

@heatmap

@pattern
ISFR questions repeat the same four facts: the **national percentage**, the **largest by area** (MP), the **largest by percentage** (Mizoram) and the **largest very dense forest** (Arunachal). Mizoram alone has been the answer five times. Many keys are pinned to an older report, so look up the year in the question and use the note to update.
@end

@next
Likely next angles: **ISFR 2023** numbers (21.76%, 25.17%, 4,992 sq km mangroves), the **carbon stock** (about 7,285 million tonnes) against the NDC sink target of **2.5-3 billion tonnes of CO2** equivalent, **forest fire** hotspots (Uttarakhand, Odisha), **bamboo** cover, and **agroforestry** (trees outside forests).
@end
