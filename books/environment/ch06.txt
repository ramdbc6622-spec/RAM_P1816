@chapter 6 | Climate Change Conferences, Treaties and Action Plans

@intro
This chapter covers the international and national response to climate change: the Earth Summit and **Agenda 21**, the **UNFCCC** and its COPs (Kyoto, Paris), climate finance (**Green Climate Fund**, **GEF**), market mechanisms (**CDM**, carbon credits, carbon tax), the match-the-treaty questions (Cartagena, Stockholm, Montreal, Kyoto), and India's **National Action Plan on Climate Change** with its eight missions. It contains **{pyqs} PYQs and {ones} one-liners**.
@end

@pyq U.P.P.C.S.(Pre) 2012
The United Nations Convention on Climate Change ratified by more than 50 countries became effective on –
(a) March 21, 1994
(b) May 21, 1995
(c) June 21, 1996
(d) June 21, 1999
@ans (a)
@end

The **UNFCCC** was signed at **Rio (1992)** and came into force on **21 March 1994**. It has **198 parties**, and its secretariat is in **Bonn**. It divides countries into **Annex I** (industrialised), **Annex II** (rich countries that must provide finance) and **non-Annex I** (developing, including India), under the principle of "**Common But Differentiated Responsibilities**" (CBDR).

@one U.P.P.C.S. (Pre) 2021
The concept of carbon credit' originated from
@ans Kyoto protocol
@end

The **Kyoto Protocol** set up three market mechanisms: **Emissions Trading**, **Joint Implementation** and the **Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)**. Under the CDM, rich countries **invest** in clean projects in developing countries and earn **Certified Emission Reductions** (1 credit = 1 tonne of CO2). The Paris Agreement's **Article 6** replaces them. India launched its own **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme** in **2023**.

@trap
**Kyoto = 1997 (COP3)**, **Paris = 2015 (COP21)**, **GCF = Cancun 2010**, not Durban. **Earth Summit + 5 = 1997** (New York). **Carbon trading = Kyoto**, not Montreal, which is about the ozone layer. **Nuclear power is not an NAPCC mission.** **Cartagena = biosafety**, **Stockholm = POPs**.
@end

@heatmap

@pattern
Conference questions have moved from **dates and places** (Rio, Kyoto, Paris, COP numbers) towards **mechanisms** (GCF, GEF, CDM, carbon credits, net zero), with recent papers (2019-2025) asking statement-type questions. NAPCC questions ask about the **year (2008)** and **which mission is not included** (nuclear power).
@end
